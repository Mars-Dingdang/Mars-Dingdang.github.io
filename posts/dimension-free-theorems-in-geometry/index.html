Dimension Free Theorems in Geometry

Mars-Dingdang · Original notes dated April 13, 2026

Many geometric bounds depend on the ambient dimension. For example, Carathéodory’s theorem guarantees that any point in the convex hull of a set in \(\mathbb R^n\) can be represented using at most \(n+1\) of its points. When the set lies in the Euclidean unit ball, allowing an error of \(\varepsilon\) reduces the required number to \(\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil\), independently of the dimension. This approximation is useful in the analysis of algorithms for high-dimensional data.

In these notes we use Maurey’s empirical method to obtain that approximation, count the resulting cover of a polytope, and turn the cover into a volume bound. Then we use the Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma to preserve distances and approximate supporting half-spaces under a linear map.

Carathéodory’s theorem

Convex combinations and convex hulls

A convex combination of \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\in\mathbb R^n\) is a sum \(\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i x_i\) with \(\lambda_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i=1\). The convex hull of \(T\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) is the set of all its finite convex combinations:

\[\operatorname{conv}(T)= \left\{\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i x_i: x_i\in T,\ \lambda_i\ge0,\ \sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i=1,\ k\ge1\right\}.\]

Theorem (Carathéodory). Every \(x\in\operatorname{conv}(T)\) is a convex combination of at most \(n+1\) points from \(T\).

Proof. Write \(x=\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i x_i\), dropping zero coefficients. If \(m>n+1\), the \(m-1\) vectors \(x_2-x_1,\ldots,x_m-x_1\) are linearly dependent. Choose coefficients \(c_2,\ldots,c_m\), not all zero, with \(\sum_{i=2}^m c_i(x_i-x_1)=0\). Setting \(\mu_1=-\sum_{i=2}^m c_i\) and \(\mu_i=c_i\) for \(i\ge2\) gives

\[\sum_{i=1}^m\mu_i=0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^m\mu_i x_i=0.\]

Since the \(\mu_i\) sum to zero and are not all zero, some are positive. Take

\[\alpha=\min_{\mu_i>0}\frac{\lambda_i}{\mu_i}.\]

The new coefficients \(\lambda_i-\alpha\mu_i\) are nonnegative and sum to one. They represent the same \(x\), and at least one is zero. Repeat until at most \(n+1\) coefficients remain. \(\square\)

The bound is tight. The origin and the \(n\) standard basis vectors form an \(n\)-simplex. Each point in its interior has all \(n+1\) barycentric coordinates positive.

Approximate Carathéodory by independent sampling

Theorem. Suppose \(T\subseteq B_2^n\), the unit Euclidean ball. For every \(x\in\operatorname{conv}(T)\) and every integer \(k\ge1\), there are \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\in T\), with repetition allowed, such that

\[\left\|x-\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^k x_j\right\|_2\le \frac1{\sqrt{k}}.\]

Proof. Write \(x=\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i z_i\) and define a random vector \(Z\) by sampling \(z_i\) with probability \(\lambda_i\). Then \(\mathbb E Z=x\). Let \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k\) be independent copies of \(Z\). Cross terms have expectation zero, so

\[\begin{aligned} \mathbb E\left\|\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^k Z_j-x\right\|_2^2 &=\frac1{k^2}\sum_{j=1}^k\mathbb E\|Z_j-x\|_2^2\\ &=\frac{\mathbb E\|Z\|_2^2-\|x\|_2^2}{k} \le \frac1k. \end{aligned}\]

At least one sample attains squared error at most \(1/k\). This is Maurey’s empirical method. \(\square\)

Figure 1. The empirical mean approximates a point in a three-dimensional convex hull.

Tightness. For \(1\le k\le n\), take \(T=\{e_1,\ldots,e_n\}\) and \(x=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n e_i\). If \(u=\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_j x_j\) is a convex combination from \(T\), merge coefficients on repeated points. Write \(u=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i e_i\), with \(\sum_i a_i=1\) and at most \(k\) nonzero entries. Then

\[\|x-u\|_2^2 =\sum_{i=1}^n\left(a_i-\frac1n\right)^2 =\sum_{i=1}^n a_i^2-\frac1n \ge \frac1k-\frac1n.\]

The last step is Cauchy–Schwarz. Equality is attained by placing mass \(1/k\) on \(k\) distinct basis vectors. Thus even arbitrary convex weights cannot improve the \(k^{-1/2}\) order when \(n\) is large compared with \(k\).

Covering a polytope and bounding its volume

Let \(P\subseteq B_2^n\) be a polytope with \(N\) vertices, and let \(k\ge1\) be an integer. Every point in \(P\) is within \(1/\sqrt{k}\) of the average of \(k\) vertices. Taking all ordered choices gives at most \(N^k\) centers. The order of the vertices has no effect on the average. Counting the possible multiplicities \(c_1,\ldots,c_N\ge0\) with \(\sum_i c_i=k\) gives a smaller upper bound by stars and bars:

\[M\le \binom{N+k-1}{k}.\]

Several multiplicity vectors can give the same center, which only reduces the cover size.

Figure 2. A cover of a three-dimensional polytope by balls centered on averages of vertices.

For a cover by \(M\) balls of radius \(1/\sqrt{k}\), volume subadditivity gives

\[\left(\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(P)}{\operatorname{Vol}(B_2^n)}\right)^{1/n} \le \frac{M^{1/n}}{\sqrt{k}}.\]

A first volume bound. With \(M=N^k\),

\[\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(P)}{\operatorname{Vol}(B_2^n)} \le \left(3\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{n}}\right)^n.\]

For \(N=1\) the volume is zero. When \(n<9\log N\), the right side exceeds one, which suffices since \(P\subseteq B_2^n\). Otherwise take \(k=\lceil n/(2\log N)\rceil\). Then

\[k^{-1/2}\le\sqrt{\frac{2\log N}{n}},\qquad N^{k/n}\le e^{1/2}N^{1/n}\le e^{11/18}.\]

Now \(\sqrt2\,e^{11/18}<3\). \(\square\)

A combinatorial estimate. For integers \(1\le k\le m\),

\[\left(\frac{m}{k}\right)^k \le\binom{m}{k} \le\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{m}{j} \le\left(\frac{em}{k}\right)^k.\]

For the first inequality, each factor \((m-i)/(k-i)\) in the product for \(\binom{m}{k}\) is at least \(m/k\). For the last, take \(t=k/m\in(0,1]\). Since \(t^j\ge t^k\) for \(j\le k\),

\[\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{m}{j} \le t^{-k}(1+t)^m \le t^{-k}e^{mt}=\left(\frac{em}{k}\right)^k.\]

Theorem (Carl–Pajor volume bound). If \(P\) is full-dimensional and has \(N\) vertices, then

\[\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(P)}{\operatorname{Vol}(B_2^n)} \le \left(23\sqrt{\frac{\log(eN/n)}{n}}\right)^n.\]

If \(P\) is lower-dimensional, its \(n\)-dimensional volume is zero.

Proof. Full dimension implies \(N\ge n+1\). Write \(L=\log(eN/n)>1\). If \(n<2L\), the bound follows from \(P\subseteq B_2^n\). Otherwise take \(k=\lceil n/(2L)\rceil\). Then \(n/(2L)\le k\le n/L\le n\), so

\[M\le\binom{N+k-1}{k}\le\left(\frac{2eN}{k}\right)^k.\]

Using \(2eN/k\le 4eNL/n\) and \(k/n\le1/L\), we obtain

\[\log(M^{1/n}) \le \frac{\log 4+L+\log L}{L} \le \log 4+1+1/e<2.76.\]

Here \(\log L/L\le1/e\) for \(L\ge1\). Thus \(M^{1/n}<16\), while \(1/\sqrt{k}\le\sqrt{2L/n}\). Their product is bounded by \(23\sqrt{L/n}\). \(\square\)

The Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma

Theorem. For \(0<\varepsilon<1\) and \(n\ge2\), any set of \(n\) points in \(\mathbb R^d\) admits a linear map \(f\colon\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^k\) such that

\[(1-\varepsilon)\|x-y\|_2^2 \le\|f(x)-f(y)\|_2^2 \le(1+\varepsilon)\|x-y\|_2^2\]

for every pair of points, provided \(k\ge8\log n/\varepsilon^2\).

Proof. Let \(A\in\mathbb R^{k\times d}\) have independent \(\mathcal N(0,1/k)\) entries. For any fixed nonzero \(v\),

\[X=\frac{k\|Av\|_2^2}{\|v\|_2^2}\sim\chi_k^2.\]

Its moment generating function is \((1-2t)^{-k/2}\) for \(t<1/2\). Applying Markov’s inequality to \(e^{tX}\) with \(t=(r-1)/(2r)\) gives the upper tail when \(r>1\) and the lower tail when \(0<r<1\):

\[\begin{aligned} \Pr(X\ge kr)&\le e^{-\frac k2(r-1-\log r)} &&(r>1),\\ \Pr(X\le kr)&\le e^{-\frac k2(r-1-\log r)} &&(0<r<1). \end{aligned}\]

To obtain the stated constant, set \(s=\operatorname{arctanh}(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\) and choose \(f(x)=Ax/\sqrt{s}\). The two thresholds for \(X/k\) are \(r_\pm=s(1\pm\varepsilon)\). We have \(r_+>1\) and \(0<r_-<1\) by bounding the integrand in \(\operatorname{arctanh}\varepsilon=\int_0^\varepsilon(1-t^2)^{-1}\,dt\). Since \(r_+-r_-=2s\varepsilon=\log(r_+/r_-)\), the two rate terms \(r_\pm-1-\log r_\pm\) are equal. Each is their average:

\[s-1-\log s-\tfrac12\log(1-\varepsilon^2)\ge\tfrac12\varepsilon^2.\]

Thus each fixed pair fails with probability at most \(2e^{-k\varepsilon^2/4}\). A union bound over the \(\binom n2\) pairs gives a failure probability at most \((n-1)/n<1\) when \(k\ge8\log n/\varepsilon^2\). \(\square\)

Figure 3. The two Gaussian projections shown here go from 3D through 283D to 2D. The final panel illustrates the geometry. The theorem’s dimension bound determines when a uniform distortion guarantee applies.

For fixed distortion, the order \(\log n\) is necessary for some sets of \(n\) points, even if the embedding may be nonlinear. Exercise 4 gives a packing argument.

Approximate supporting half-spaces after projection

Suppose \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body containing the unit ball, and \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\in\partial K\). The points lie on supporting hyperplanes in the original space. Under a suitable linear map to dimension \(O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(2m))\), we can place the images inside a convex body whose boundary stays within \(O((1+D^2)\varepsilon)\) of each image, where \(D=\operatorname{diam}K\). We can also keep the projected supporting directions with a comparable one-sided error.

Projection onto a closed convex set. If \(C\) is nonempty, closed and convex, every \(y\) has a unique closest point \(x\in C\). A minimizing sequence for \(\|y-z\|_2\) is bounded and has a subsequence converging in \(C\). Uniqueness follows because for two different minimizers, the squared distance to their midpoint is strictly less than the common minimum. The closest point is characterized by

\[\langle y-x,z-x\rangle\le0\qquad(z\in C).\]

Indeed, for \(0\le t\le1\), the segment \(x+t(z-x)\) belongs to \(C\). The right derivative of \(\|y-x-t(z-x)\|_2^2\) at \(t=0\) must be nonnegative, which gives the inequality. Conversely, expanding \(\|y-z\|_2^2\) shows it is at least \(\|y-x\|_2^2\).

Supporting hyperplane at a boundary point. Given \(x\in\partial K\), take \(y_\ell\notin K\) with \(y_\ell\to x\). If \(z_\ell\) is the closest point in \(K\) to \(y_\ell\), then \(\|y_\ell-z_\ell\|_2\le\|y_\ell-x\|_2\), so \(z_\ell\to x\). A subsequence of the unit vectors \((z_\ell-y_\ell)/\|z_\ell-y_\ell\|_2\) converges to a unit vector \(n\). The projection inequality then gives \(\langle z-x,n\rangle\ge0\) for every \(z\in K\). Thus \(n\) is an inward normal of a supporting hyperplane through \(x\).

Preservation argument. Let \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\). Choose a unit inward normal \(n_i\) at each \(x_i\) and put \(y_i=x_i+n_i\). Apply Johnson–Lindenstrauss to the set \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_m,y_1,\ldots,y_m\}\). Write \(x'_i=f(x_i)\) and \(n'_i=f(n_i)\). For each \(i,j\) the three squared distances of the triangle \(x_i,y_i,x_j\) are preserved within factors \(1\pm\varepsilon\). Polarization gives

\[\begin{aligned} \langle n'_i,x'_j-x'_i\rangle &=\tfrac12\bigl(\|f(y_i-x_i)\|_2^2+\|f(x_j-x_i)\|_2^2-\|f(y_i-x_j)\|_2^2\bigr)\\ &\ge (1+\varepsilon)\langle n_i,x_j-x_i\rangle -\varepsilon\bigl(1+\|x_j-x_i\|_2^2\bigr)\\ &\ge -(1+D^2)\varepsilon. \end{aligned}\]

Set \(C_D=1+D^2\). Each \(x'_j\) is strictly inside every half-space

\[H_i=\{z:\langle z-x'_i,n'_i\rangle\ge -(C_D+1)\varepsilon\}.\]

Intersect the \(H_i\) with a closed ball large enough to contain all \(x'_j\) in its interior. This gives a compact convex body \(K'\) with nonempty interior. In direction \(n'_i\), its actual supporting plane is attained at the minimum of \(z\mapsto\langle z-x'_i,n'_i\rangle\) on \(K'\). That minimum lies between \(-(C_D+1)\varepsilon\) and \(0\) because \(K'\subseteq H_i\) and \(x'_i\in K'\). Since \(\|n'_i\|_2\ge\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}>1/2\), the plane is within \(2(C_D+1)\varepsilon\) of \(x'_i\). The segment from \(x'_i\) to its orthogonal projection onto the plane meets \(\partial K'\) no later than it meets the plane. This yields the desired distance bound. Normalizing \(n'_i\) to unit length changes the half-space error by at most a factor of two. \(\square\)

Exercises

1. Independent sets by the probabilistic method

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be an undirected graph and \(d(v)\) the degree of \(v\).

  1. Prove that \(G\) has an independent set of size at least \(\sum_{v\in V}1/(d(v)+1)\). Hint. Order the vertices uniformly at random and keep those that come before every neighbor.
  2. If \(|V|=n\) and \(|E|=m\), deduce a lower bound of \(n^2/(2m+n)\).

2. Where the mass of a Euclidean ball lies

Let \(X\) be uniform in \(B_2^n\), so \(\Pr(X\in A)=\operatorname{Vol}(A)/\operatorname{Vol}(B_2^n)\) for measurable \(A\subseteq B_2^n\).

  1. Prove that the shell \(1-5/n\le\|X\|_2\le1\) contains at least \(99\%\) of the mass.
  2. Prove that \(\mathbb E\|X\|_2=n/(n+1)\).

3. The chi-square distribution and random projection

Let \(X=\sum_{i=1}^k Z_i^2\), where the \(Z_i\) are independent standard Gaussians.

  1. Compute \(\mathbb EX\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(X)\).
  2. Derive \(\mathbb E e^{tX}=(1-2t)^{-k/2}\) for \(t<1/2\).
  3. Use the moment generating function and the logarithm’s Taylor bound to show that for \(0<\varepsilon<1\),
\[\Pr\bigl(|X-\mathbb EX|>\varepsilon\mathbb EX\bigr) \le2\exp\left(-\frac{k}{4}(\varepsilon^2-\varepsilon^3)\right).\]

Show also that the probability is at most \(2e^{-Ck\varepsilon^2}\) for an absolute \(C>0\) over this range. Hint. For the second bound, use \(\varepsilon-\log(1+\varepsilon)=\int_0^\varepsilon t/(1+t)\,dt\).
4. Combine this estimate with a union bound to derive the Johnson–Lindenstrauss dimension order.

4. A dimension lower bound by packing

  1. If \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\in\mathbb R^k\) satisfy \(1<\|z_i-z_j\|_2\le2\) for all \(i\ne j\), show that \(n\le5^k\). Hint. Pack disjoint open balls of radius \(1/2\) around the points inside a ball of radius \(5/2\).
  2. Find \(n\) vectors in \(\mathbb R^n\) for which no map into \(\mathbb R^k\) preserves every distance to relative error \(0.01\) when \(k<\tfrac12\log n\). Hint. Try the standard basis vectors and use part 1.

5. Characterizing a closest point

Given a nonempty closed convex set \(C\) and \(x\in C\), prove that \(x\) is the projection of \(y\) onto \(C\) if and only if \(\langle y-x,z-x\rangle\le0\) for all \(z\in C\). Try proving both directions before checking the argument above.

6. A deterministic greedy construction

Let \(T\subseteq B_2^n\) and \(x\in\operatorname{conv}(T)\). Construct a sequence \(x_1,x_2,\ldots\in T\) such that for every \(k\ge1\), its empirical mean \(p_k=k^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^k x_i\) satisfies

\[\|x-p_k\|_2^2\le 4/k.\]

Hint. Set \(e_k=x-p_k\). Express \(x\) as a convex combination from \(T\) and choose \(x_{k+1}\) with \(\langle e_k,x-x_{k+1}\rangle\le0\). Then bound the change in the squared norm of \(ke_k\).

The choice of a point by a linear objective also appears in Frank–Wolfe optimization. In this exercise the iterates retain a sparse convex representation.

7. Sparse strategies in a large zero-sum game

Let \(A\in[-1,1]^{m\times n}\) be the payoff matrix and let \(y\in\Delta_n\) be a mixed strategy for the column player. Here \(\Delta_n=\{u\in\mathbb R^n:u_i\ge0,\ \sum_i u_i=1\}\). Given \(\varepsilon>0\), show that a strategy \(\widehat y\in\Delta_n\) supported on at most

\[k=\left\lceil\frac{2\log(2m)}{\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil\]

columns can satisfy \(\|A\widehat y-Ay\|_\infty\le\varepsilon\). Hint. Sample columns independently according to \(y\). Apply Hoeffding’s inequality coordinate by coordinate and count the possible payoff deviations.

8. Coresets for an SVM margin

Let \(P,Q\subset B_2^n\) be two point sets whose convex hulls attain a minimum distance \(\gamma>0\). Prove that for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there are subsets \(P'\subseteq P\) and \(Q'\subseteq Q\), each of size at most \(\lceil4/\varepsilon^2\rceil\), such that

\[\gamma\le\min_{\substack{p\in\operatorname{conv}(P')\\q\in\operatorname{conv}(Q')}}\|p-q\|_2 \le\gamma+\varepsilon.\]

Hint. Approximate one closest pair \(p_\star,q_\star\) by averages of \(k\) points from the respective sets, with \(k=\lceil4/\varepsilon^2\rceil\), and apply the triangle inequality. The result gives a dimension-independent coreset for the optimal margin value.
