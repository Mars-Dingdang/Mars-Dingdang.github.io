Computer Graphics

ACG 01: Introduction to Computer Graphics

A rendered image is the result of a computation. Geometry specifies what is in the scene. Materials and light determine surface appearance, the camera selects a viewpoint, and the display reproduces the resulting samples. Separating these inputs makes it possible to explain a visual effect and test its cause.

From a 3D scene to a 2D image

Write a scene as \(\mathcal S=(G,M,L,C)\), with geometry, materials, lights, and a camera. Rendering computes \(I=\mathcal R(\mathcal S)\). Changing only \(C\) isolates the effects of perspective and visibility. Changing only \(M\) or \(L\) exposes how reflectance and illumination interact. Computer vision often reverses part of this computation by estimating scene properties from images.

For a pinhole camera looking along positive \(Z\), put the virtual image plane at \(Z=f\). The ray through a point \(P=(X,Y,Z)\) meets the image plane at \((fX/Z,fY/Z,f)\). Both coordinates follow from scaling the vector \(P\) by \(f/Z\).

\[(x,y)=\left(f\frac{X}{Z},f\frac{Y}{Z}\right).\]

Multiplying all coordinates by any positive \(\lambda\) leaves the image point unchanged. One view constrains a point to a ray. Given a second calibrated camera and a corresponding image point, intersecting two rays adds a depth constraint. Closely spaced cameras yield nearly parallel rays for distant objects, so small observation errors can still produce large depth errors.

Modeling, motion, and display

A triangle mesh stores positions and the connections between them. It can attach surface normals, texture coordinates, and materials to its vertices or faces. An implicit model defines a surface through a zero set, such as \(F(x)=\|x-c\|^2-r^2=0\) for a sphere. This sphere function makes containment a sign test. A mesh makes it straightforward to enumerate and transform the triangles of a surface. The rendering and modeling operations determine which representation is useful.

Animation adds time. When an animator specifies two positions at times \(t_0,t_1\), linear interpolation gives

\[p(t)=(1-s)p_0+sp_1,\qquad s=\frac{t-t_0}{t_1-t_0}.\]

The derivative is \((p_1-p_0)/(t_1-t_0)\) throughout the interval. Matching the endpoints of consecutive segments creates continuous positions while still allowing an abrupt velocity change. Skeletal animation propagates joint transformations down a hierarchy and uses them to move skin vertices. Physical simulation advances positions and velocities using forces and constraints. A spring with rest length \(\ell_0\) stores \(\tfrac12 k(\ell-\ell_0)^2\) of potential energy and exerts the restoring force \(-k(\ell-\ell_0)\) along its extension coordinate.

A framebuffer holds discrete image samples before display encoding. Let \(g\) map a linear intensity to a display code. Equal-area averaging belongs in linear space, so the encoded average of intensities \(a,b\) is \(g((a+b)/2)\). For a nonlinear \(g\), this generally differs from \((g(a)+g(b))/2\). The same distinction matters in texture filtering and alpha compositing.

Two rendering algorithms

Rasterization projects primitives and enumerates the samples they cover. At an opaque pixel, a depth test keeps the nearest submitted fragment. Triangle projection, coverage, and shading can therefore be divided into pipeline stages.

Ray casting starts at the camera and queries the scene for the first hit along each sample ray. A second ray toward a point light tests whether the surface receives direct light. Recursive or sampled rays can estimate mirror reflections, refractions, and indirect light. Both rasterization and ray tracing compute visibility, but their loop orders differ: rasterization starts with primitives, while ray casting starts with samples.

Exercises

  1. Stereo conditioning. Two identical rectified cameras share one image plane orientation. The right camera is displaced by \(b\) along \(X\). Derive depth from the measured disparity and quantify the first-order error when the disparity changes by \(\delta d\).
Answer

With the same focal length \(f\), the projected coordinates are \(x_L=fX/Z\) and \(x_R=f(X-b)/Z\). Thus \(d=x_L-x_R=fb/Z\), so \(Z=fb/d\) and \(\delta Z\approx-fb\,\delta d/d^2\). At fixed baseline and focal length, the absolute depth error grows quadratically with distance for constant disparity error.

  1. Repair a velocity discontinuity. An object must go from \(0\) to \(1\) in one second, with zero velocity at each endpoint. Find the cubic of lowest possible degree that satisfies the constraints, and calculate its peak velocity.
Answer

The conditions at zero give \(p(t)=at^3+bt^2\). At one, \(a+b=1\) and \(3a+2b=0\), so \(p(t)=3t^2-2t^3\). Its derivative \(6t(1-t)\) reaches \(1.5\) at \(t=1/2\). A quadratic with derivative zero at two distinct points would have a derivative identically zero, so the cubic is minimal.

  1. Diagnose incorrect compositing. With display encoding \(g(L)=\sqrt L\), a pixel straddles equal areas of white and black. Compare the displayed linear intensity from averaging codes with the physically correct result.
Answer

Averaging codes gives \((g(0)+g(1))/2=0.5\). The display decodes that to \(L=0.25\). Equal illuminated areas average to \(L=0.5\), which should be encoded as \(1/\sqrt 2\). Code-space averaging makes this edge too dark.
