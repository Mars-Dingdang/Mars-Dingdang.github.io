ACG 02: Curves, Meshes, and Implicit Surfaces

A parametric surface answers “where is the point at these coordinates?” An implicit field answers “what is the value at this position?” A triangle mesh answers questions about connectivity. These different query types explain why a graphics system often uses several geometry representations for the same object.

Constructing a Bézier curve

Given control points \(P_0,\ldots,P_n\), repeatedly interpolate adjacent points. Set \(P_i^{(0)}=P_i\) and

\[P_i^{(k)}(t)=(1-t)P_i^{(k-1)}(t)+tP_{i+1}^{(k-1)}(t).\]

Expanding the recursion reveals how many ways each control point reaches the output. A path from \(P_i\) takes \(i\) right branches and \(n-i\) left branches, so the coefficient is \(\binom ni t^i(1-t)^{n-i}\). This gives the Bernstein form

\[B(t)=\sum_{i=0}^n P_i b_{i,n}(t),\qquad b_{i,n}(t)=\binom ni t^i(1-t)^{n-i}.\]

On \([0,1]\), the weights are nonnegative and sum to \((t+1-t)^n=1\). Hence the curve lies in the convex hull of the control polygon, and affine maps commute with evaluation. Differentiating a basis polynomial yields \(b'_{i,n}=n(b_{i-1,n-1}-b_{i,n-1})\), with out-of-range terms set to zero. Reindexing the derivative gives

\[B'(t)=n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(P_{i+1}-P_i)b_{i,n-1}(t).\]

For two consecutive cubics \(P\) and \(Q\), position continuity requires \(P_3=Q_0\). If their durations are \(h_P,h_Q\), matching derivatives in global time requires \((P_3-P_2)/h_P=(Q_1-Q_0)/h_Q\). Merely matching the direction of their nonzero tangent vectors gives geometric \(G^1\) continuity. Hermite curves encode tangents directly, while B-splines use basis functions with local support to control long curves.

Subdivision with de Casteljau

Each level of interpolation produces the endpoints of a smaller control polygon on both sides of the split. The following algorithm returns the control polygons for the intervals \([0,t]\) and \([t,1]\), each reparametrized locally to \([0,1]\).

The two outputs share their endpoint \(B(t)\). The algorithm takes quadratic time in the degree and linear auxiliary space. To tessellate for display, split in object space, project each child control polygon, and compare its control points with the chord between its endpoints. For a child whose clip-space weights are all positive, the projected curve is a rational Bézier curve with nonnegative normalized weights. It stays in the convex hull of the projected control points, so their greatest distance to the chord segment bounds the curve’s distance as well. Under perspective projection, splitting the ordinary 2D polygon alone would compute a different curve.

For tensor-product surfaces, evaluate one set of control curves along \(u\) and then evaluate the resulting control polygon along \(v\):

\[S(u,v)=\sum_{i,j}P_{ij}b_{i,m}(u)b_{j,n}(v).\]

At a regular point, the cross product \(S_u\times S_v\) is perpendicular to both tangent directions and gives a normal after normalization.

Point clouds and meshes

A point cloud stores samples, optionally with normals or appearance attributes. To sample an indexed mesh uniformly over area, select a face with probability equal to its fraction of total area. Draw uniform \(u,v\in[0,1]\). If \(u+v>1\), replace them by \(1-u,1-v\). The two halves of the square now map onto the same parameter triangle by an area-preserving transformation. The affine map \(A+u(B-A)+v(C-A)\) preserves uniformity inside the selected face.

A triangle uses its index order to determine normal direction via \((B-A)\times(C-A)\). In a two-manifold triangle mesh, each interior edge belongs to two faces and each boundary edge to one. Each vertex link must also form a single cycle or path. Two triangles touching only at a vertex obey the edge-count test but give that shared vertex a disconnected neighborhood.

Subdivision, simplification, and deformation

Loop subdivision replaces each triangle with four smaller triangles and updates vertex positions. For interior edge \(AB\) and opposite vertices \(C,D\), the new edge point is \(\tfrac38(A+B)+\tfrac18(C+D)\). Catmull–Clark uses input faces, edges, and vertices to create new quadrilateral faces. Creases and boundaries require their own stencils.

Quadric error metrics simplify meshes by prioritizing inexpensive edge collapses. The squared distance to a plane \(n^\mathsf T x+d=0\) with unit \(n\) is \((p^\mathsf T\tilde x)^2\) for \(p=(n,d)\) and \(\tilde x=(x,1)\). Accumulating these penalties gives \(Q=\sum pp^\mathsf T\). Merge the two endpoint quadrics when collapsing an edge. Partitioning \(Q\) yields

\[E(x)=x^\mathsf T A x+2b^\mathsf T x+c,\qquad \nabla E(x)=2Ax+2b.\]

The optimum is \(-A^{-1}b\) when \(A\) is invertible. In a locally flat region, the error may leave the two tangent directions unconstrained. Evaluate the original endpoints or optimize along their segment. Reject collapses that invalidate connectivity or flip protected faces.

As-rigid-as-possible deformation minimizes discrepancies between new edges and locally rotated original edges,

\[E=\sum_i\sum_{j\in N(i)}w_{ij}\|(p'_i-p'_j)-R_i(p_i-p_j)\|^2,\qquad R_i\in SO(3).\]

A common rigid transform \(p_i'=Rp_i+t\) gives zero error. The translation cancels in edge differences and every \(R_i\) can equal \(R\). Fix some handle positions, alternate estimating the rotations and solving the linear system for positions, and stop when the energy changes little. Free-form deformation shapes space itself by interpolating displacements on a control lattice.

Implicit geometry

A sphere signed distance field is \(F(x)=\|x-c\|-r\). For a differentiable regular level set, differentiating \(F(x(s))=0\) along a surface curve gives \(\nabla F\cdot x'(s)=0\). Thus the gradient supplies a normal candidate. With negative values inside, a point lies in the union if \(\min(F_A,F_B)<0\), in the intersection if \(\max(F_A,F_B)<0\), and in the interior of the difference if \(F_A<0<F_B\). A combined field preserves these sign tests while its values can lose the exact distance interpretation. Sample the field on a grid and extract a triangulated isosurface when an explicit surface is needed.

Exercises

  1. Unequal segment durations. A cubic ends at \(P_3=(2,1)\) and approaches it from \(P_2=(1,0)\). It takes three seconds. The next cubic takes one second. Find its first two control points for \(C^1\) continuity.
Answer

Take \(Q_0=P_3=(2,1)\). Matching \(3(P_3-P_2)/3\) to \(3(Q_1-Q_0)/1\) gives \(Q_1=(7/3,4/3)\). Choosing \(Q_1=(3,2)\) matches direction but triples the global speed at the join.

  1. Singular QEM. The contributing planes are \(x=0\) and \(y=0\). Find all minimizers and then find the optimal point on the segment from \((1,0,1)\) to \((0,1,3)\).
Answer

The energy is \(x^2+y^2\), so every point on the \(z\) axis is an unconstrained minimizer. Along the segment, \(E(s)=(1-s)^2+s^2\) is minimized at \(s=1/2\), giving \((1/2,1/2,2)\) with energy \(1/2\).

  1. CSG distance. Let \(F_A(x)=|x|-2\) and \(F_B(x)=|x-1|-2\) on the real line. Show why the minimum field preserves the union’s sign while failing to give exact signed distance at \(x=0.5\).
Answer

The solid intervals are \([-2,2]\) and \([-1,3]\), giving union \([-2,3]\). At \(x=0.5\), both fields are \(-1.5\), but the nearest union boundary is \(2.5\) units away. The exact signed distance is \(-2.5\).
