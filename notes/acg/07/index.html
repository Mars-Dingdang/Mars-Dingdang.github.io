ACG 07: Ray Intersection, Acceleration, and Light Transport

Ray tracing separates two questions. An intersection query finds the first surface hit along a ray. A transport calculation determines the radiance that surface sends toward the camera. Primary visibility, shadows, and mirror reflections use the same intersection machinery with different directions and parameter intervals.

Primary and secondary rays

A ray is \(r(t)=o+td\), evaluated on a permitted interval. With unit direction \(d\), \(t\) is distance. For a shadow query from \(x\) to a point light at \(\ell\), choose direction \(\ell-x\) and check for an occluder strictly between \(t=0\) and \(t=1\). A closest-hit query returns the smallest permitted intersection, while an any-hit query can stop after finding an occluder.

Whitted-style recursion adds mirror reflections and refractions to direct lighting. For a unit incident direction \(d\) pointing toward a surface with unit normal \(n\), reflecting flips its normal component and keeps the tangent component, yielding \(d_r=d-2(d\cdot n)n\). Snell’s law gives the transmitted direction when refraction is possible. Recursion depth bounds the workload and truncates longer paths. Diffuse interreflection involves many incident directions rather than a single secondary ray.

Sphere, plane, and triangle intersections

For a sphere centered at \(c\) with radius \(R\), substitute \(o+td\) into \(\|r(t)-c\|^2=R^2\) and put \(m=o-c\):

\[(d\cdot d)t^2+2(m\cdot d)t+(m\cdot m-R^2)=0.\]

The discriminant tests for real roots. Pick the smallest root in the allowed interval, which may be the larger quadratic root when the origin is inside the sphere. For a general implicit surface \(F(x)=0\), substitution similarly reduces the search to the scalar equation \(F(o+td)=0\).

For a plane through \(A\) with normal \(n\), solve \(n\cdot(o+td-A)=0\) to obtain \(t=n\cdot(A-o)/(n\cdot d)\). A zero denominator indicates a parallel ray. The numerator then determines whether the ray lies in the plane.

Möller–Trumbore combines triangle plane intersection and barycentric tests. For \(e_1=B-A\) and \(e_2=C-A\), solve

\[[-d\quad e_1\quad e_2] \begin{pmatrix}t\\u\\v\end{pmatrix}=o-A.\]

Set \(s=o-A\), \(p=d\times e_2\), \(q=s\times e_1\), and \(D=e_1\cdot p\). Cramer’s rule gives \(u=(s\cdot p)/D\), \(v=(d\cdot q)/D\), and \(t=(e_2\cdot q)/D\). For a nonsingular system, accept only \(u,v\ge0\), \(u+v\le1\), and a parameter \(t\) within the ray interval.

Acceleration structures

To intersect an axis-aligned bounding box, solve \(b_i^-\le o_i+t d_i\le b_i^+\) for each axis with \(d_i\ne0\). The two crossing parameters have to be sorted when the direction is negative. Intersect the resulting intervals with the ray interval:

\[t_{\mathrm{enter}}=\max_i t_i^-, \qquad t_{\mathrm{exit}}=\min_i t_i^+.\]

When \(d_i=0\), the ray’s fixed coordinate either excludes the whole ray or imposes no additional limit. Allow a singleton interval to retain tangential intersections and zero-thickness bounds.

A uniform grid stores primitive references in cells. Its DDA traversal advances to the next crossing by comparing the candidate times for each axis, updating a crossed axis by its cell width divided by the absolute direction component. A kd-tree partitions space recursively and clips the ray interval at each split. A primitive may overlap both halves. A BVH partitions primitives instead. Its child bounding boxes may overlap, but the primitive groups in a basic BVH are disjoint. PBRT, “Bounding Volume Hierarchies”

Implement near-first BVH traversal

A stack stores candidate nodes and their box-entry parameters. The current best hit provides a shrinking far bound.

The first child popped has the nearest box entry, so its hits can prune farther boxes early. Box entry is only a lower bound. Returning immediately from the first hit leaf can be wrong when boxes overlap. For a shadow query that needs any opaque hit, an immediate return is valid.

Radiometry

Quantity Definition Unit
Radiant flux \(\Phi\) Energy per unit time W
Radiant intensity \(I\) Flux per unit solid angle W/sr
Irradiance \(E\) Incoming flux per unit surface area W/m²
Radiance \(L\) Flux per projected area and unit solid angle W/(m²·sr)

A patch at distance \(r\) with area \(dA\) and normal angle \(\theta\) subtends \(dA\cos\theta/r^2\) at a point source facing its front. This gives irradiance \(I\cos\theta/r^2\). Integrating general incoming radiance gives

\[E(x)=\int_{\Omega^+(n)} L_i(x,\omega)(n\cdot\omega)\,d\omega.\]

For uniform incident radiance \(L\), write \(d\omega=\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi\). The hemisphere integral of the cosine is \(\pi\), so the irradiance is \(\pi L\). PBRT, “Radiometry”

The rendering equation

A BRDF \(f_r\) relates outgoing radiance to irradiance from one incoming direction. Conservation of energy bounds the directional reflected fraction,

\[\int_{\Omega^+(n)} f_r(x,\omega_i,\omega_o) (n\cdot\omega_o)\,d\omega_o\le1 .\]

The Lambertian function \(\rho/\pi\) integrates to \(\rho\). For an opaque reflective surface, adding emission and summing reflected contributions gives the rendering equation,

\[L_o(x,\omega_o)=L_e(x,\omega_o)+ \int_{\Omega^+(n)}f_r(x,\omega_i,\omega_o) L_i(x,\omega_i)(n\cdot\omega_i)\,d\omega_i .\]

Under the outward direction convention, if the ray along \(\omega_i\) reaches \(y\) first through vacuum, then \(L_i(x,\omega_i)=L_o(y,-\omega_i)\). This couples outgoing radiance at different surfaces. Path tracing estimates the integral by sampling directions. Given a sample density \(p(\omega)\) in solid-angle units, each sample contributes its integrand divided by \(p(\omega)\), assuming the density is positive wherever the integrand is nonzero.

Exercises

  1. Prune a BVH without discarding the nearest hit. Child boxes intersect the ray over \([2,8]\) and \([4,6]\). The first child reports a primitive hit at \(t=7\). Can the traversal skip the second child? What if the first hit were at \(t=3\)?
Answer

With a hit at \(7\), the second box begins at \(4\) and may contain a closer surface. With a hit at \(3\), its lower bound \(4\) is already farther away, so the second child can be skipped.

  1. Keep the exit root. A ray starts at \((0,0,1)\) with direction \((0,0,2)\) inside a sphere of radius \(3\) at the origin. Find the valid hit and its world-space distance.
Answer

Solve \((1+2t)^2=9\), giving \(t=1,-2\). The forward hit is at \((0,0,3)\), two world units from the ray origin. The direction is nonunit, so the valid parameter \(1\) is not the distance.

  1. Choose a sampling measure. For a Lambertian patch with constant incoming radiance \(L\), compare one-sample estimates from cosine-weighted hemisphere sampling and uniform hemisphere sampling.
Answer

With \(f_r=\rho/\pi\) and \(p(\omega)=\cos\theta/\pi\), the factor of cosine cancels and every sample gives \(\rho L\). Uniform hemisphere sampling uses \(p=1/(2\pi)\) and yields \(2\rho L\cos\theta\). Since a uniform hemisphere draw has \(\cos\theta\) uniform on \([0,1]\), the latter has mean \(\rho L\) and variance \((\rho L)^2/3\).
