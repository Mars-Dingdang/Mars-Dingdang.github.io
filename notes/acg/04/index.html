ACG 04: Projection and Learning on 3D Geometry

Perspective projection maps three-dimensional positions to two-dimensional image coordinates. Single-view reconstruction asks what can be recovered after that mapping. Camera geometry identifies the missing constraints, and learning supplies priors about plausible scenes.

Orthographic and perspective projection

Use a right-handed camera with forward axis \(-z\). Let \(n,f\) be the positive near and far distances, \(d=-z\), and set normalized depth to \([-1,1]\) with near mapping to \(-1\). Orthographic projection centers and scales each coordinate independently. For horizontal interval \([l,r]\), impose the two endpoint values to get \(x_{\mathrm{ndc}}=(2x-r-l)/(r-l)\). The same linear construction applies to vertical position and depth.

Perspective instead projects along rays from the camera center. Let \(\phi\) be the vertical field of view and \(a\) the width-to-height ratio. The near plane’s half-height is \(n\tan(\phi/2)\), and similar triangles scale the near-plane intersection by \(n/d\). After normalization,

\[x_{\mathrm{ndc}}=\frac{x}{ad\tan(\phi/2)},\qquad y_{\mathrm{ndc}}=\frac{y}{d\tan(\phi/2)}.\]

Choose clip \(w_c=d\), making the homogeneous division produce the desired scale. Assume \(z_c=Az+B\) so normalized depth is \(-A+B/d\). Evaluating at \(d=n,f\) yields \(A=-(f+n)/(f-n)\) and \(B=-2fn/(f-n)\). For \(s=1/\tan(\phi/2)\), the projection matrix is

\[P=\begin{pmatrix} s/a&0&0&0\\0&s&0&0\\ 0&0&-\frac{f+n}{f-n}&-\frac{2fn}{f-n}\\ 0&0&-1&0 \end{pmatrix}.\]

The derivative of depth with respect to \(d\) is \(2fn/((f-n)d^2)\). Uniform quantization of projected depth therefore corresponds to coarser distance resolution farther away. A smaller near plane consumes more of the representable depth range near the eye. After clipping and division, a \(W\times H\) top-left-origin viewport maps NDC to \(x_s=W(x_{\mathrm{ndc}}+1)/2\) and \(y_s=H(1-y_{\mathrm{ndc}})/2\).

Clip before the perspective divide

The visible clip-space region satisfies six linear half-space inequalities, such as \(w+x\ge0\) for the left boundary. An edge whose endpoints \(A,B\) lie on opposite sides of a clipping plane \(g(C)=0\) intersects it at

\[t=\frac{g(A)}{g(A)-g(B)},\qquad C=(1-t)A+tB.\]

Sutherland–Hodgman clipping processes one plane at a time. For each polygon edge, emit an intersection if membership changes and emit the next vertex if it is inside. Interpolate ordinary perspective-correct vertex attributes with the same \(t\) before dividing by \(w\). After all six planes, triangulate the clipped polygon and divide its retained vertices by \(w\). Performing clipping in screen space after division would mishandle edges that cross the eye plane.

Choosing a representation for 3D learning

Representation Useful computation Main tradeoff
Multiview images Share a 2D encoder and aggregate features across views Occluded surfaces depend on viewpoint coverage
Dense voxels Apply regular 3D kernels Doubling resolution per axis requires eight times as many cells
Sparse voxels and octrees Process occupied cells or subdivide selected regions Neighbor lookup requires a spatial index or cross-level handling
Point sets Process scan samples directly Sampling density varies and connectivity is absent
Meshes Use face and edge structure and output a surface directly A deformable template generally retains its topology
Implicit fields Query occupancy or distance at arbitrary coordinates Surface extraction requires additional queries

Datasets differ in the signals available for training. ShapeNet studies object-level geometry, PartNet adds part hierarchies, ScanNet provides indoor observations, and HOI4D records interactions over time. An evaluation should state whether the input is a complete shape, a partial scan, or a changing scene.

Set aggregation and local structure

PointNet shares a feature extractor \(h\) across points, uses per-channel maxima, and applies an output head \(\gamma\):

\[F(\{p_1,\ldots,p_m\})=\gamma\left(\max_i h(p_i)\right).\]

Reordering the points reorders the feature rows, leaving each maximum unchanged. A perturbation that changes only points below all existing maxima can likewise leave the output unchanged. The same property limits the ability to infer density or local arrangements. PointNet++ selects local neighborhoods in the metric space, encodes relative coordinates in each neighborhood, and repeats the procedure at larger scales. PointNet · PointNet++

A simple implementation can use farthest-point sampling to choose neighborhood centers. Track the smallest squared distance from each point to any selected center, update the distance array after adding each center, and take its maximum for the next center. This costs \(O(mN)\) for \(m\) centers and \(N\) points, with \(O(N)\) memory. Gather neighbors for each center using a radius or nearest-neighbor query. Subtract the center coordinates so each local feature extractor sees the geometry of the patch.

Single-view reconstruction objectives

Scaling an object uniformly about the pinhole leaves its projected coordinates unchanged. Depth supervision can accommodate this ambiguity by comparing the shape of the predicted depth map up to one per-image scale. With \(\delta_i=\log\hat z_i-\log z_i\), minimize the squared error after adding a global offset \(\alpha\) to the predicted log depths. Differentiation gives \(\alpha=-\bar\delta\), and the minimized loss is

\[\frac1m\sum_i\delta_i^2-\left(\frac1m\sum_i\delta_i\right)^2 .\]

Point sets lack a canonical ordering, so a reconstruction can use the bidirectional squared Chamfer objective,

\[D(P,Q)=\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{p\in P}\min_{q\in Q}\|p-q\|^2 +\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{q\in Q}\min_{p\in P}\|q-p\|^2.\]

The first term measures prediction accuracy and the second term measures coverage. Both are permutation invariant. Because a target can be reused as the closest neighbor of many different predictions, the objective gives limited control over sample density. Mesh prediction can instead deform a template, sample surface points for data terms, and regularize neighboring vertices and faces. A fixed template constrains changes to topology.

Exercises

  1. Find precision loss. Under this projection, compare the world-space depth increments corresponding to an equal, infinitesimal NDC increment at \(d=2n\) and at \(d=20n\). Assume both are inside the frustum.
Answer

Invert \(dz_{\mathrm{ndc}}/dd=2fn/((f-n)d^2)\) locally. The distance increment scales as \(d^2\), so the farther sample’s increment is \(100\) times as large.

  1. Clip a segment. Against the left clip plane \(w+x=0\), clip the segment from \(A=(-3,0,0,1)\) to \(B=(0,2,0,2)\). Give the intersection and its screen \(y\) in NDC.
Answer

The signed values are \(g(A)=-2\) and \(g(B)=2\), giving \(t=1/2\). The intersection is \((-1.5,1,0,1.5)\), whose normalized \(y\) is \(2/3\). Interpolating the already-divided \(y\) values at the same parameter would give \(1/2\) and the wrong geometry.

  1. Separate ordinary and scale-invariant depth errors. The target depths are \((1,2,4)\) and predictions are \((2,4,4)\). Compute the optimal multiplicative correction and scale-invariant loss.
Answer

The log discrepancies are \((\log 2,\log 2,0)\) with mean \(2\log 2/3\). The scale correction is \(2^{-2/3}\), and the remaining variance is \(2(\log 2)^2/9\).
