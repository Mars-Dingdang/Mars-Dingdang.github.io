ML 12: Robust Learning and Randomized Smoothing

Derive exact robust margins for linear classifiers and prove a Gaussian-smoothing certificate for bounded input changes.

A shift in the input changes the probability assigned to either side of the classifier’s decision boundary.

Robust risk and the envelope derivative

A perturbation set \(\Delta\) models allowable input changes. The worst-case training objective is

\[\min_\theta\mathbb E_{X,Y}\!\left[\max_{\delta\in\Delta}\ell(f_\theta(X+\delta),Y)\right].\]

An \(\ell_\infty\) ball bounds each coordinate change, whereas an \(\ell_2\) ball bounds total squared magnitude. Robustness depends on the selected input metric.

Let \(F(\theta)=\max_{z\in\Delta}g(\theta,z)\), with \(\Delta\) compact and \(g,\nabla_\theta g\) continuous. Write \(M(\theta)\) for its set of maximizers. For any \(z\in M(\theta)\), evaluating it at \(\theta+tv\) gives the lower bound \(\liminf_{t\downarrow0}(F(\theta+tv)-F(\theta))/t\ge \nabla_\theta g(\theta,z)^\top v\). For the upper bound choose a maximizer \(z_t\) at \(\theta+tv\). Since \(F(\theta)\ge g(\theta,z_t)\), the difference quotient is bounded above by the directional change in \(g\) at \(z_t\). Compactness gives a convergent subsequence of maximizers, continuity puts its limit in \(M(\theta)\), and the uniform first-order remainder yields

\[F'(\theta;v)=\max_{z\in M(\theta)}\nabla_\theta g(\theta,z)^\top v.\]

With a unique maximizer this gives the gradient through the outer parameters. A numerical lower bound on the maximum loss can reveal failures, while a certificate needs an upper bound on the maximum or a direct invariance argument.

Linear robust margins are exact

For a linear score \(w^\top x+b\) and \(y\in\{-1,1\}\), let \(m=y(w^\top x+b)\). The dual-norm identity gives

\[\min_{\|\delta\|_p\le r}y(w^\top(x+\delta)+b)=m-r\|w\|_q,\quad 1/p+1/q=1.\]

Hölder’s inequality gives the lower bound, and compactness of the unit ball and the definition of the dual norm give equality. The whole closed ball is classified correctly when \(m>r\|w\|_q\). At \(p=2\) the limiting radius agrees with the geometric distance from the SVM lecture.

The Gaussian smoothing proof

For base classifier \(f\), smooth by class probabilities \(p_c(x)=P(f(x+Z)=c)\) with \(Z\sim N(0,\sigma^2 I)\) and choose the most likely class. Suppose the leading class \(A\) has probability at least \(\underline p_A\) at \(x\) and any other class has probability at most \(\overline p_B<\underline p_A\).

Let \(P=N(x,\sigma^2I)\), \(Q=N(x+\delta,\sigma^2I)\) and \(L(z)=dQ/dP(z)\). Given fixed \(P\)-mass, a set of low likelihood ratio minimizes \(Q\)-mass. To prove it, choose \(H=\{L\le c\}\) with the desired mass, and compare any set \(E\) of equal mass:

\[Q(E)-Q(H)=\int (\mathbf1_E-\mathbf1_H)(L-c)\,dP\ge0.\]

Both products on the symmetric difference are nonnegative. Reversing the threshold proves the upper-bound version. For nonzero \(\delta\), the logarithm of \(L\) is \(\delta^\top(z-x)/\sigma^2-\|\delta\|^2/(2\sigma^2)\), monotone along \(\delta\). Consequently the extremal sets are halfspaces in that direction. Projection onto \(\delta/\|\delta\|\) shifts the mean by \(\|\delta\|\) and preserves standard deviation \(\sigma\). For any class \(A\) event with the stated lower bound, its \(Q\)-mass is at least

\[\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\underline p_A)-\|\delta\|/\sigma),\]

and every other class has \(Q\)-mass at most \(\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\overline p_B)+\|\delta\|/\sigma)\). Therefore the prediction is unchanged for

\[\|\delta\|<\frac{\sigma}{2}\big(\Phi^{-1}(\underline p_A)-\Phi^{-1}(\overline p_B)\big).\]

The guarantee applies to the smoothed classifier. If probabilities are estimated by sampling, substitute simultaneous valid confidence bounds into the formula. A larger \(\sigma\) increases the prefactor but may shrink the class-probability gap.

Challenge

For a binary halfspace base classifier with nonzero normal \(w\) and a point at positive signed geometric distance \(d\), derive its smoothed probability and show that the certified radius is exact.

Show solution

Gaussian projection along \(w/\|w\|\) has variance \(\sigma^2\), so the smoothed positive probability is \(\Phi(d/\sigma)\) and the negative probability is \(\Phi(-d/\sigma)\). The certificate radius is \(\sigma(\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(d/\sigma))-\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(-d/\sigma)))/2=d\). Smoothing leaves this halfspace’s decision boundary in place, because positive probability exceeds \(1/2\) precisely on its positive side. A shift along the negative normal of magnitude greater than \(d\) therefore switches the prediction.

Second challenge. Find the exact Gaussian smoothing certificate for a binary base classifier whose decision boundary is a hyperplane.

Show solution to the second challenge

Let \(h(z)=\mathbf1\{w^\top z\ge b\}\) and \(w\ne0\). With isotropic Gaussian noise, the positive class probability at \(x\) is \(p=\Phi((w^\top x-b)/(\sigma\|w\|))\). The runner-up has probability \(1-p\), so on the positive side the certificate is \(\sigma\Phi^{-1}(p)=(w^\top x-b)/\|w\|\). Moving against the unit normal reaches the hyperplane at exactly this distance. The averaged classifier retains the original boundary.
