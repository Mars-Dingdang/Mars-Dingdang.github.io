ML 06: Linear Models, Sparsity, and Compressed Sensing

Derive least squares, perceptron convergence, logistic curvature, and soft thresholding, then prove when sparse signals can be recovered.

The diamond has a minimizer on a coordinate axis. The disk has a minimizer with two nonzero coordinates.

Least squares and ridge

For a design matrix \(X\) and responses \(y\), stationarity of \(\|Xw-y\|^2/2\) gives \(X^\top Xw=X^\top y\). The fitted response is the orthogonal projection of \(y\) onto \(\operatorname{col}(X)\), even when several parameter vectors attain it. Ridge adds \(\lambda\|w\|^2/2\). With \(\lambda>0\) and thin SVD \(X=U\Sigma V^\top\),

\[w_\lambda=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y =\sum_i\frac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_i^2+\lambda}\langle u_i,y\rangle v_i.\]

This follows by diagonalizing \(X^\top X\) in the right-singular basis. In a direction with small positive singular value, least squares uses a large inverse gain \(1/\sigma_i\). Ridge shrinks that gain. The normal equation is a linear system that can be solved without forming the inverse.

For the mean loss \(\ell(w)=\|Xw-y\|_2^2/(2n)\), differentiation gives \(\nabla\ell=X^\top(Xw-y)/n\) and \(\nabla^2\ell=X^\top X/n\). Adding \(\lambda\|w\|_2^2/2\) shifts every Hessian eigenvalue by \(\lambda\), so the new objective is \(\lambda\)-strongly convex. Its exact gradient step is \(w^+=(1-\eta\lambda)w-\eta\nabla\ell(w)\). Shrinking after a data-gradient step also scales that gradient, producing a difference of \(\eta^2\lambda\nabla\ell(w)\). An intercept can be absorbed by a constant feature. Remove it from the regularizer if the intercept should remain unpenalized.

Let \(R(w)\) be either \(\|w\|_2^2/2\) or \(\|w\|_1\). If \(w_\lambda\) minimizes \(\ell+\lambda R\) with \(\lambda\ge0\), it minimizes \(\ell\) over \(R(w)\le R(w_\lambda)\), since a feasible point with smaller loss would improve the penalized objective. Conversely, for a radius \(c>0\), \(w=0\) is strictly feasible for \(R(w)\le c\). Slater’s condition gives a multiplier \(\lambda\ge0\) with \(0\in\nabla\ell(w)+\lambda\partial R(w)\) and \(\lambda(R(w)-c)=0\), so a constrained minimizer also minimizes the penalized loss. For \(\lambda_2>\lambda_1\), add the two penalized optimality inequalities with their competitors swapped to get \((\lambda_2-\lambda_1)(R(w_{\lambda_2})-R(w_{\lambda_1}))\le0\). The origin constraint \(c=0\) may require a limiting penalty.

Why the perceptron terminates on separated data

Take labels \(\pm1\), norms \(\|x_i\|\le R\), and a unit vector \(w^\star\) with \(y_i(w^\star)^\top x_i\ge\gamma>0\). Starting at zero, update \(w\gets w+y_ix_i\) on a mistake or tie. After \(M\) updates, \((w^\star)^\top w_M\ge M\gamma\) by summing the projected increments. Each update also satisfies

\[\|w+y_ix_i\|^2=\|w\|^2+2y_iw^\top x_i+\|x_i\|^2\le\|w\|^2+R^2.\]

Thus \(M\gamma\le\|w_M\|\le R\sqrt M\), proving \(M\le(R/\gamma)^2\). A bias is handled by augmenting the vectors with a constant feature and recomputing the radius and margin.

Logistic likelihood and cross-entropy

For \(y_i\in\{0,1\}\), a Bernoulli model uses \(p_i=\sigma(s_i)\), where \(s_i=w^\top x_i\). Its negative log likelihood equals \(\log(1+e^{s_i})-y_i s_i\). Since \(\sigma'(s)=\sigma(s)(1-\sigma(s))\),

\[\nabla_w\ell_i=(p_i-y_i)x_i,\qquad \nabla_w^2\ell_i=p_i(1-p_i)x_ix_i^\top.\]

The Hessian is positive semidefinite and bounded by \(x_ix_i^\top/4\). The loss equals cross-entropy. At a fixed input, cross-entropy between the true Bernoulli parameter \(q\) and prediction \(p\) is \(H(q)+D_{\rm KL}(q\|p)\), minimized at \(p=q\) by nonnegativity of KL. For separable training data the unregularized empirical loss approaches zero along a growing separating direction and has no finite minimizer.

For general probability vectors, subtracting the entropy \(-\sum_iq_i\log q_i\) from the cross-entropy \(-\sum_iq_i\log p_i\) gives \(\sum_{i:q_i>0}q_i\log(q_i/p_i)\). If \(p_i=0<q_i\) the divergence is infinite. Otherwise \(\log z\le z-1\) at \(z=p_i/q_i\) gives \(D_{\rm KL}(q\|p)\ge1-\sum_{i:q_i>0}p_i\ge0\). Equality forces \(p=q\). For a one-hot class \(c\), the derivative of \(-\log p_c\) with respect to \(p_c\) is \(-1/p_c\). Differentiating through softmax yields the logit gradient \(p-q\). The binary threshold \(p=1/2\) corresponds to score zero. On a two-element probability vector the lecture’s alternative \(\ell_1\) loss is \(2|p-y|\).

LASSO and proximal gradient

With \(X^\top X=I\), the LASSO objective \(\|Xw-y\|^2/2+\lambda\|w\|_1\) separates into \(\tfrac12(u-z)^2+\lambda|u|\) with \(z=(X^\top y)_j\). Stationarity gives \(u=z-\lambda\) when \(u>0\) and \(u=z+\lambda\) when \(u<0\). At zero, the subgradient interval contains zero exactly when \(|z|\le\lambda\). Therefore

\[S_\lambda(z)=\operatorname{sign}(z)(|z|-\lambda)_+.\]

For general \(X\), minimize the first-order loss model plus a quadratic proximity penalty and the \(\ell_1\) term. Completing the square gives the proximal update \(w^+=S_{\eta\lambda}(w-\eta X^\top(Xw-y))\) coordinatewise. The same threshold determines which coordinates become exactly zero.

Sparse recovery and restricted geometry

From measurements \(y=Aw\), basis pursuit solves \(\min_z\|z\|_1\) subject to \(Az=y\). Uniform unique recovery of every \(s\)-sparse \(w\) is equivalent to the null-space property

\[\|h_S\|_1<\|h_{S^c}\|_1\qquad(0\ne h\in\ker A,\ |S|\le s).\]

Sufficiency follows from \(\|w+h\|_1\ge\|w\|_1-\|h_S\|_1+\|h_{S^c}\|_1>\|w\|_1\) for \(S=\operatorname{supp}(w)\). If the inequality fails, take \(w=-h_S\) and competitor \(h_{S^c}=w+h\). Both have the same measurements and the competitor has norm no greater.

The order-\(k\) restricted isometry property (RIP) asks that \((1-\delta_k)\|z\|^2\le\|Az\|^2\le(1+\delta_k)\|z\|^2\) for every \(k\)-sparse vector. The difference of two \(s\)-sparse candidates is \(2s\)-sparse. If both have residual at most \(\varepsilon\) and \(\delta_{2s}<1\), their distance is at most \(2\varepsilon/\sqrt{1-\delta_{2s}}\) by the lower RIP inequality and the triangle inequality. Exact measurements give uniqueness within the sparse class. This argument concerns sparse candidates. A basis-pursuit guarantee needs to control differences that may be dense.

\(2s\) RIP can recover dense but compressible signals">Why the order-\(2s\) RIP can recover dense but compressible signals

Suppose \(A\) has order-\(2s\) RIP constant \(\delta<1/(1+\sqrt2)\) and \(\hat x\) solves basis pursuit from \(Ax\). Let \(h=\hat x-x\), and let \(S_0\) index the \(s\) largest coordinates of \(x\) in magnitude. Since \(\|\hat x\|_1\le\|x\|_1\), the triangle inequality on \(S_0\) and its complement gives

\[\|h_{S_0^c}\|_1\le\|h_{S_0}\|_1+2\|x_{S_0^c}\|_1.\]

Break \(S_0^c\) into blocks \(S_1,S_2,\ldots\) of size \(s\) in decreasing order of \(|h_i|\), except possibly the final block. Each entry in \(S_j\) is bounded by the average absolute entry in \(S_{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\), so \(\sum_{j\ge2}\|h_{S_j}\|\le\|h_{S_0^c}\|_1/\sqrt s\).

For disjointly supported, unit, \(s\)-sparse vectors \(u,v\), the sum and difference are \(2s\)-sparse and have squared norms \(2\). Polarization and the two-sided RIP give

\[4|\langle Au,Av\rangle| =\left|\|A(u+v)\|_2^2-\|A(u-v)\|_2^2\right|\le4\delta.\]

Rescaling gives \(|\langle Au,Av\rangle|\le\delta\|u\|\|v\|\) for nonunit vectors. Set \(H=S_0\cup S_1\). Because \(Ah=0\), \(\|Ah_H\|^2=-\sum_{j\ge2}\langle Ah_H,Ah_{S_j}\rangle\). Split \(h_H\) into its two blocks. The bound \(\|h_{S_0}\|+\|h_{S_1}\|\le\sqrt2\|h_H\|\) then gives

\[(1-\delta)\|h_H\|^2 \le\sqrt2\delta\|h_H\|\sum_{j\ge2}\|h_{S_j}\|.\]

With \(\rho=\sqrt2\delta/(1-\delta)<1\) and \(\sigma=\|x_{S_0^c}\|_1/\sqrt s\), these estimates give \(\|h_H\|\le\rho(\|h_H\|+2\sigma)\) and hence \(\|h_H\|\le2\rho\sigma/(1-\rho)\). Finally \(\|h\|\le\|h_H\|+\sum_{j\ge2}\|h_{S_j}\|\le2\|h_H\|+2\sigma\), so

\[\|\hat x-x\|_2\le\frac{2(1+\rho)}{1-\rho}\frac{\|x_{S_0^c}\|_1}{\sqrt s}.\]

The right-hand side vanishes for an \(s\)-sparse signal. With measured noise the equality \(Ah=0\) is replaced by a residual bound and contributes an additional error term.

Why Gaussian measurements work

For a matrix with independent \(N(0,1/m)\) entries, any fixed unit vector has squared measured norm distributed as \(\chi^2_m/m\). Its deviation outside \(1\pm t\) has probability at most \(2e^{-mt^2/8}\) for \(0<t<1\). Indeed, the moment-generating function of \(\chi^2_m\) is \((1-2s)^{-m/2}\). Markov’s inequality with \(s=t/(2(1+t))\) on the upper side gives \(\exp[-m(t-\log(1+t))/2]\). With \(s=-t/(2(1-t))\) on the lower side it gives \(\exp[-m(-t-\log(1-t))/2]\). The quantities \(t-\log(1+t)=\int_0^t u/(1+u)\,du\) and \(-t-\log(1-t)=\int_0^t u/(1-u)\,du\) are at least \(t^2/4\) and \(t^2/2\), respectively.

On one support of size \(k\), take a maximal \(1/4\)-separated set of unit vectors. Maximality gives a \(1/4\)-net, and the disjoint radius-\(1/8\) balls about its points all fit in a radius-\(9/8\) ball, giving at most \(9^k\) points by volume. For \(B=A_S^\top A_S-I\), if all net values satisfy \(|v^\top Bv|\le\delta/2\), then \(\|B\|\le\delta\): approximate a maximizing unit vector by a net vector and bound the quadratic-form difference by \(2\|B\|/4\). The union bound over at most \((ed/k)^k\) supports and \(9^k\) net points per support gives \(m\gtrsim\delta^{-2}(k\log(ed/k)+\log(1/\alpha))\) for failure probability at most \(\alpha\).

In particular, the preceding tail estimate shows the failure probability is at most \(2\binom dk9^ke^{-m\delta^2/32}\), so \(m\ge32\delta^{-2}[k\log(9ed/k)+\log(2/\alpha)]\) suffices. The slides state the sufficient condition \(m\ge216k\log(120d/(\alpha\delta))/\delta^2\). Both describe the standard Gaussian scaling. Applying the fixed-vector estimate directly to normalized pairwise differences of a finite set proves the Gaussian Johnson–Lindenstrauss bound with \(O(\delta^{-2}(\log n+\log(1/\alpha)))\) measurements for \(n\) points.

For a fixed orthogonal basis \(\Psi\), the transpose of each row of \(A\Psi\) is a linear transform of a jointly Gaussian vector, with covariance \(\Psi^\top(I/m)\Psi=I/m\). Zero covariance gives independence for Gaussian coordinates, and the rows remain independent. Thus \(A\Psi\) has the same law as \(A\), giving the same high-probability guarantee for signals sparse in that basis.

Recovery with a nonlinear generator

Let \(S=\{G(z):z\in\mathbb R^k\}\) and \(y=Ax^\star+\eta\). Suppose \(A\) has a lower secant bound \(\|A(u-v)\|\ge(2/3)\|u-v\|\) for all \(u,v\in S\). Choose a nearest comparator \(u\) in the closure of \(S\), which exists because the distance to \(x^\star\) has bounded sublevel sets. The secant and residual inequalities extend to the closure by continuity. For this fixed comparator, suppose \(\|A(u-x^\star)\|\le(5/3)\|u-x^\star\|\). If \(\hat x=G(\hat z)\) has measured residual within \(\varepsilon\) of the infimum, then

\[\tfrac23\|\hat x-u\| \le\|A(\hat x-u)\| \le2\|A(u-x^\star)\|+2\|\eta\|+\varepsilon.\]

Divide by \(2/3\) and add \(\|u-x^\star\|\) to obtain a reconstruction error at most \(6\inf_z\|G(z)-x^\star\|+3\|\eta\|+2\varepsilon\).

For a fixed depth-\(D\) ReLU generator of layer width at most \(c\), the Gaussian sample bound is order \(kD\log c\). Each feasible activation pattern gives an affine output on the corresponding latent region. A layer cuts such a region by at most \(c\) hyperplanes in \(\mathbb R^k\), creating at most \(\sum_{j=0}^k{c\choose j}\) pieces (add one hyperplane and count its induced regions inductively). Across all layers the number of pieces is at most \((c+1)^{O(kD)}\). Secants lie in a union of subspaces of dimension at most \(2k+1\), one for each pair of affine images. Apply Gaussian concentration and the net argument to each and union bound. This yields the lower secant inequality with probability \(1-e^{-\Omega(m)}\) for sufficiently many measurements. The upper comparator bound also follows from fixed-vector concentration. The argument assumes approximate minimization of the measured residual and keeps that error explicit.

Challenges

Problem 1

Suppose all columns of \(A\) have unit Euclidean norm and mutual coherence \(\mu=\max_{i\ne j}|a_i^\top a_j|\). Prove basis pursuit recovers every \(s\)-sparse vector if \((2s-1)\mu<1\).

Show solution

For \(h\in\ker A\), take the inner product of \(\sum_jh_ja_j=0\) with \(a_i\). This gives \(|h_i|\le\mu\sum_{j\ne i}|h_j|\), hence \((1+\mu)|h_i|\le\mu\|h\|_1\). Sum over any \(S\) of size at most \(s\) to obtain \(\|h_S\|_1\le s\mu\|h\|_1/(1+\mu)<\|h\|_1/2\). This is exactly the strict null-space inequality for nonzero \(h\).

Problem 2

For least squares penalized by \(\lambda_1\|w\|_1+\lambda_2\|w\|^2/2\), derive the solution when \(X^\top X=I\). Prove uniqueness for arbitrary \(X\) when \(\lambda_2>0\).

Show solution

Put \(z=X^\top y\). Each coordinate minimizes \((1+\lambda_2)u^2/2-z_ju+\lambda_1|u|\), giving \(S_{\lambda_1}(z_j)/(1+\lambda_2)\) by its three subgradient cases. For arbitrary \(X\), the quadratic Hessian obeys \(X^\top X+\lambda_2I\succeq\lambda_2I\). Adding the convex penalty preserves strong convexity, and coercivity gives existence, hence the minimizer is unique.

Paper challenge · Basis pursuit with noisy measurements

In the noisy model \(y=Ax+e\) with \(\|e\|_2\le\varepsilon\), replace exact feasibility by \(\|Az-y\|_2\le\varepsilon\) and minimize \(\|z\|_1\). Prove a bound with the same \(\delta_{2s}<\sqrt2-1\) as the noiseless theorem, and give explicit coefficients of \(\varepsilon\) and the sparsity tail. This develops the noisy recovery theorem from Candès’s RIP analysis. Candès, “The Restricted Isometry Property and Its Implications for Compressed Sensing”

Show paper solution

Both \(x\) and \(\widehat x\) are feasible, so \(\|Ah\|_2\le2\varepsilon\) for \(h=\widehat x-x\). The cone inequality and block tail estimates above still hold because \(x\) is a valid competitor in the norm minimization. Keep \(H=S_0\cup S_1\), let \(\sigma=\|x_{S_0^c}\|_1/\sqrt s\), and write \(Ah_H=Ah-\sum_{j\ge2}Ah_{S_j}\). Taking its inner product with \(Ah_H\) gives

\[(1-\delta)\|h_H\|^2 \le2\varepsilon\sqrt{1+\delta}\|h_H\| +\sqrt2\delta\|h_H\|\sum_{j\ge2}\|h_{S_j}\|.\]

The first term uses Cauchy–Schwarz and the upper RIP bound. Divide by \(\|h_H\|\) when positive, use \(\sum_{j\ge2}\|h_{S_j}\|\le\|h_H\|+2\sigma\), and take \(\rho=\sqrt2\delta/(1-\delta)\). Rearranging gives \(\|h_H\|\le[2\varepsilon\sqrt{1+\delta}/(1-\delta)+2\rho\sigma]/(1-\rho)\). This bound also holds when the head is zero. Adding the tail yields

\[\|\widehat x-x\|_2\le \frac{4\sqrt{1+\delta}}{1-(1+\sqrt2)\delta}\,\varepsilon +\frac{2(1+\rho)}{1-\rho}\,\sigma .\]

The two terms separately control measurement noise and the departure from exact sparsity.
