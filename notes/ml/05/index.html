ML 05: PAC Learning and VC Dimension

Prove finite-class sample bounds and compute VC dimension by combining explicit realizations with impossible labelings.

Opposite vertices of the square share a label. The two convex hulls intersect at its center.

Realizable and agnostic PAC guarantees

In binary classification, \(R(h)\) is the probability of error on an independent test draw. Realizable PAC learning assumes some hypothesis in the class has zero risk. The learner must output a predictor with risk at most \(\varepsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\) over the training sample. Agnostic PAC learning permits arbitrary labels and asks for risk within \(\varepsilon\) of \(\inf_{h\in\mathcal H}R(h)\).

For any fixed hypothesis of risk greater than \(\varepsilon\), the chance it makes no errors on \(n\) independent examples is \((1-R(h))^n\le e^{-n\varepsilon}\). A union bound over a finite class and the existence of a consistent hypothesis give the realizable ERM bound

\[n\ge\frac{\log|\mathcal H|+\log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon}.\]

For the agnostic case, two-sided Hoeffding and a union bound give

\[P\!\left(\sup_{h\in\mathcal H}|\widehat R(h)-R(h)|>\varepsilon/2\right) \le 2|\mathcal H|e^{-n\varepsilon^2/2}.\]

On the complementary event, \(R(\widehat h)\le\widehat R(\widehat h)+\varepsilon/2\le\widehat R(h)+\varepsilon/2\le R(h)+\varepsilon\) for every \(h\). Take the infimum. A population-risk comparison with the Bayes predictor separates approximation error, \(\inf_{\mathcal H}R-R(h^\star)\), from the learner’s excess above the best in its class.

For a no-free-lunch example, select labels independently and uniformly for each point in a uniform domain of \(2n\) points, then draw \(n\) training examples. At least half the domain remains unseen. Conditional on the data, each unseen label remains an independent fair coin, giving expected risk at least \(1/4\) over the labeling and sample. Some deterministic labeling attains that lower bound in expectation over training draws. A random risk in \([0,1]\) satisfies \(\mathbb E R\le 1/8+(7/8)P(R\ge1/8)\), so for that labeling the learner has risk at least \(1/8\) with probability at least \(1/7\). If \(\mathcal H\) has infinite VC dimension, we can choose a shattered \(2n\)-point set for every \(n\). Its deterministic labeling is then realizable in \(\mathcal H\), so the bound contradicts any distribution-free PAC sample complexity at, for example, \(\varepsilon=1/9,\delta=1/8\).

Infinite classes can have small effective complexity

For thresholds \(h_t(x)=\mathbf1\{x\ge t\}\) on the line, two ordered points cannot receive \((1,0)\), but one point can receive either label. The VC dimension is 1. A single interval shatters two points, while three ordered points cannot receive \((1,0,1)\), so its dimension is 2.

For a realizable threshold at \(t^\star\), any consistent empirical minimizer has zero sample points in its error interval. If the empirical threshold is larger than \(t^\star\), the error interval is an initial segment of \([t^\star,\infty)\); if it is smaller, the error interval is a terminal segment of \((-\infty,t^\star)\). On each side with enough mass, choose the closest fixed segment of mass at least \(\varepsilon\), using the probability quantile and including the boundary atom if needed to reach \(\varepsilon\). Every error interval on that side of mass greater than \(\varepsilon\) contains this segment. Therefore the probability of any consistent threshold with risk greater than \(\varepsilon\) is at most \(2(1-\varepsilon)^n\). This directly yields order \(\log(1/\delta)/\varepsilon\) samples. The same argument applies to concentric disks by replacing \(x\) with negative distance to the known center.

In \(\mathbb R^d\), an affine halfspace shatters \(d+1\) affinely independent points because an affine function interpolates arbitrary assigned values \(\pm1\). For any \(d+2\) points, the augmented vectors \((x_i,1)\) have a nontrivial linear dependence. Its coefficients sum to zero, and the positive and negative groups therefore have convex combinations representing the same point. Opposite labels on those groups cannot be realized. Thus the dimension is exactly \(d+1\).

Sauer–Shelah and the generalization bound

Let \(\Pi_d(n)\) denote the largest possible trace size on \(n\) points for VC dimension at most \(d\). Split the traces on the first \(n-1\) points into prefixes appearing at least once and prefixes that can be extended with either last label. The first family has VC dimension at most \(d\), and the second at most \(d-1\), because shattering \(d\) points with both extensions would shatter \(d+1\) points. Thus \(\Pi_d(n)\le\Pi_d(n-1)+\Pi_{d-1}(n-1)\). The base cases \(n=0\) and \(d=0\) have at most one trace. Pascal’s identity yields

\[\Pi_{\mathcal H}(n)\le\sum_{j=0}^d {n\choose j}\le(en/d)^d\qquad(1\le d\le n).\]

For the last inequality, multiply the binomial sum by \((d/n)^d\), use \((d/n)^d\le(d/n)^j\) for \(j\le d\), and bound \((1+d/n)^n\) by \(e^d\).

To see why this controls uniform convergence, add an independent ghost sample of size \(n\). For \(n\varepsilon^2\ge 2\), a hypothesis whose true and empirical risk differ by more than \(\varepsilon\) has at least a constant probability that its ghost error is within \(\varepsilon/2\) of the truth, by Chebyshev’s inequality for Bernoulli averages. The resulting two-sample event has a discrepancy above \(\varepsilon/2\). Swap the two members of each independent sample pair with a fair coin. Conditional on the pooled data, the discrepancy for any fixed trace is an average of independent signed terms bounded in magnitude by 1. Hoeffding and a union bound over at most \(\Pi_{\mathcal H}(2n)\) pooled prediction traces give

\[P\!\left(\sup_h|\widehat R(h)-R(h)|>\varepsilon\right) \le 4\Pi_{\mathcal H}(2n)e^{-n\varepsilon^2/8}\]

under the usual measurability assumptions. This gives an order \(\sqrt{(d\log(en/d)+\log(1/\delta))/n}\) bound for the empirical-population gap. The quantitative fundamental theorem stated in the course gives agnostic sample complexity \(\Theta((d+\log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^2)\) and the textbook realizable bounds \(\Omega((d+\log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon)\) and \(O((d\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon)\) in their usual nontrivial parameter range. The sharper agnostic bound uses more than the one-scale union bound above. Together with the shattered-set lower bound, finite VC dimension characterizes distribution-free PAC learnability for binary classes under the standard regularity conditions.

Challenges

Problem 1

Find the VC dimension of all subsets of the real line that are unions of at most \(k\) intervals. Permit the empty set.

Show solution

The dimension is \(2k\). Among \(2k\) ordered points, any binary labeling has at most \(k\) contiguous runs of positives. Put one interval around each positive run, excluding neighboring negative points. For \(2k+1\) ordered points the alternating labeling starting and ending with 1 has \(k+1\) positive runs. Every interval joining any two of those runs contains a negative point. That labeling is impossible on every set of \(2k+1\) points.

Problem 2

Compare the VC dimensions of \(\mathbf1\{a\sin x>0\}\), \(\mathbf1\{\sin(x+a)>0\}\), and \(\mathbf1\{\sin(ax)>0\}\).

Show solution

The amplitude class has three distinct functions and can shatter one point, giving dimension 1. Phase shifts produce open semicircles of positives. The points \(0,\pi/2\) admit all four labels. Among three distinct points, either they cannot all fit in an open semicircle, or they can be ordered within one. In the latter case any open semicircle containing the two extremes contains the short arc between them, so the middle point cannot be negative. The dimension is 2.

For frequency scaling use \(x_i=2^{i-1}\) for any \(n\). Given labels \(y_i\), put \(b_i=1-y_i\) and take \(a=2\pi(\sum_{j=1}^n b_j2^{-j}+2^{-n-1})\). The fractional part of \(ax_i/(2\pi)\) is \(b_i/2\) plus a number strictly between 0 and \(1/2\). Its sine is positive precisely when \(b_i=0\). Hence these \(n\) points are shattered for arbitrary \(n\).
