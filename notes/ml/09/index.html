ML 09: PCA, Nearest Neighbors, and Metric Learning

Prove the equivalence of PCA’s variance and reconstruction objectives, derive locality-sensitive hashing guarantees, and learn distances from labels.

Each thin segment represents a reconstruction residual. Squaring and averaging its length gives the reconstruction loss of the chosen direction.

PCA and orthogonal reconstruction

Center the columns of \(X=[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) and form \(C=XX^\top/n\). A unit vector \(v\) maximizes retained variance \(v^\top Cv\) exactly when it minimizes the mean squared reconstruction error. By orthogonality,

\[n^{-1}\sum_i\|x_i-vv^\top x_i\|^2=\operatorname{tr}(C)-v^\top Cv.\]

Writing \(v=\sum_j a_j u_j\) in the eigenbasis gives \(v^\top Cv=\sum_j\lambda_j a_j^2\) with \(\sum_j a_j^2=1\). The maximum is the largest eigenvalue. More generally, for \(k\) orthonormal columns \(V\), the retained variance is \(\sum_j\lambda_j\|V^\top u_j\|^2\). Each weight is in \([0,1]\) and their sum is \(k\), so the largest \(k\) eigenvalues give the best possible total. The omitted eigenvalues sum to the minimum residual.

For \(\lambda_1>\lambda_2\) and initial \(v_0\) with nonzero first eigencoordinate, the normalized power iteration gives a vector parallel to \(C^tv_0\). Each eigencoordinate relative to the first is multiplied by \((\lambda_j/\lambda_1)^t\), establishing convergence to the leading eigendirection. Orthogonal projection away from an exactly known leading eigenvector preserves the other eigendirections, which permits orthogonal deflation. Scaling features changes the covariance geometry, so the units of the data affect the principal directions.

More quantitatively, write \(v_0=\sum_j a_j u_j\) with \(a_1\ne0\). The tangent of the angle from \(u_1\) to the \(t\)th iterate, ignoring its sign, is at most \((\lambda_2/\lambda_1)^t\sqrt{\sum_{j\ge2}a_j^2}/|a_1|\). This follows by taking the ratio between the perpendicular component and the \(u_1\) projection of \(C^tv_0\). If \(a_1=0\) and \(a_2\ne0\), the iteration remains in \(u_1^\perp\) and can converge to \(u_2\) when \(\lambda_2\) is the simple largest eigenvalue in that subspace. With a repeated top eigenvalue it converges to the direction of the initial projection onto the whole top eigenspace, assuming that projection is nonzero.

Slides 12–23 illustrate compression using \(12\times12\) image patches. Treat each patch as a vector in \(\mathbb R^{144}\), subtract the mean patch, and keep coefficients along the chosen \(k\) principal directions. At reconstruction, multiply the coefficients by the basis and add back the mean. The relative squared reconstruction error is \(\sum_{j>k}\lambda_j/\sum_j\lambda_j\), provided total centered variance is positive. The displayed PCA basis resembles several low-frequency cosine patterns, whereas a DCT basis is fixed before seeing the image. Gaussian Johnson–Lindenstrauss projection, discussed in Lecture 6, instead preserves a finite set’s pairwise distances with high probability at dimension \(O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log n)\).

From neighbor search to locality-sensitive hashing

The \(k\) nearest labeled examples vote in a kNN classifier. Search is the separate problem of finding candidates quickly. In the \((R,cR)\) decision version, if there is an item within \(R\) of a query, an item within \(cR\) qualifies. Every returned candidate can be checked using its actual distance.

The slide sequences on Iris data (26–45) and overlapping Gaussian classes (46–68) vary \(k\) while holding the training sample fixed. With a small neighborhood, a single unusual point can create a separate decision region. A larger neighborhood stabilizes the votes in these examples, but it can also erase a small genuine cluster. A simple model explains the variance side: if the \(k\) selected labels were conditionally independent Bernoulli draws with common probability \(p\), their class proportion would have variance \(p(1-p)/k\). The common-\(p\) approximation gets worse as the neighborhood crosses regions where the true class probabilities differ.

Exact nearest-neighbor search answers an exact radius query by one distance comparison. In the other direction, suppose positive nearest distance \(r_\star\) is bracketed by known positive scales. Query independent approximate radius structures at a geometric sequence with ratio \(1+\zeta>1\) and return the smallest-distance verified answer. The first tested radius \(R\) with \(R\ge r_\star\) is at most \((1+\zeta)r_\star\), so successful analysis at that radius yields a \(c(1+\zeta)\) approximation. An earlier valid return after testing increasing radii has the same guarantee. An exact point lookup handles distance zero. The radius search in slide 72 uses doubling, which gives a factor \(2c\). Repetition over independently drawn structures reduces the per-query failure probability geometrically.

An \((R,cR,p_1,p_2)\)-sensitive hash family offers collision probability at least \(p_1\) for near points and at most \(p_2<p_1\) for far points. For Hamming distance, a uniformly chosen coordinate agrees with probability \(1-d_H(x,y)/d\). Concatenate \(k\) independent hashes so the near and far collision probabilities become at least \(p_1^k\) and at most \(p_2^k\). Then use \(L\) independent tables. A fixed near point is missed with probability at most \((1-p_1^k)^L\le e^{-Lp_1^k}\).

The tables store \(O(nL)\) point references. The success proof combines the probability of seeing a distinguished near neighbor with a budget on how many far collisions the query can examine.

Learning a useful metric

A linear representation \(Ax\) gives squared distance \((x_i-x_j)^\top A^\top A(x_i-x_j)\). Neighborhood Components Analysis (NCA) replaces a hard neighbor choice by

\[p_{ij}=\frac{e^{-\|A(x_i-x_j)\|^2}}{\sum_{k\ne i}e^{-\|A(x_i-x_k)\|^2}},\quad j\ne i,\qquad p_{ii}=0.\]

The expected number of correct leave-one-out predictions is \(\sum_i\sum_{j:y_j=y_i}p_{ij}\). Excluding self-neighbors ensures that training fits relationships between distinct examples. Another approach uses a triplet objective to prefer a positive neighbor over a negative one by a margin. Under a matrix metric \(M\succeq0\), a fixed triplet yields the hinge of an affine function of \(M\), hence a convex penalty in that variable.

Euclidean locality-sensitive hashing

For the \(p\)-stable construction at \(p=2\), let \(a\sim N(0,I_d)\) and \(b\) be uniform on \([0,w)\), independently, and hash \(h_{a,b}(x)=\lfloor(a^\top x+b)/w\rfloor\). Conditional on projection separation \(t=|a^\top(x-z)|\), the random shift places a bin boundary between the projections with probability \(\min(1,t/w)\). This follows because a boundary lies between them for exactly \(t\) out of \(w\) shift values when \(t<w\), and at least one boundary always lies between them when \(t\ge w\). The variance of \(a^\top(x-z)\) is \(\sum_i(x_i-z_i)^2\), so \(t\overset{d}{=}\|x-z\|_2|Z|\) for \(Z\sim N(0,1)\). The collision probability at distance \(r>0\) is

\[p(r)=\mathbb E\!\left[(1-r|Z|/w)_+\right] =\int_0^w \left(1-\frac tw\right)\frac2r\,\phi(t/r)\,dt,\]

where \(\phi\) is the standard Gaussian density. The second expression is the scaling calculation from slides 86–87. The same offset proof works with a symmetric \(p\)-stable random projection and \(r=\|x-z\|_p\), replacing the Gaussian density by its \(p\)-stable counterpart.
For \(0\le r_1<r_2\), use the same \(|Z|\) in both expectations. The integrand for \(r_1\) is larger on the positive-probability event \(0<|Z|<w/r_2\), and never smaller. Thus the \((R,cR)\) near and far probabilities satisfy \(p(R)>p(cR)\) for \(R>0,c>1\).

Why the LMNN objective is convex in the metric

Fix the same-class target neighbors in advance and put \(D_{ij}=(x_i-x_j)(x_i-x_j)^\top\). A positive semidefinite matrix \(M\) gives squared distance \(d_M(i,j)=\operatorname{tr}(MD_{ij})\). The large-margin nearest neighbor objective combines the sum of target-neighbor distances and a hinge penalty \(\max(0,1+d_M(i,j)-d_M(i,k))\) on differently labeled \(k\). Both distance expressions are affine in \(M\). The maximum of two affine functions is convex, sums with nonnegative weights preserve convexity, and the positive semidefinite cone is convex since each inequality \(z^\top Mz\ge0\) is linear in \(M\). Factoring \(M=T^\top T\) gives the resulting Euclidean embedding.

A bounded-work query guarantee

For a fixed query, suppose at least one data point is within \(R\). Let \(k=\lceil\log n/\log(1/p_2)\rceil\), concatenate \(k\) independent hashes per table, and let \(\rho=\log(1/p_1)/\log(1/p_2)\). A far point collides in one table with probability at most \(p_2^k\le1/n\). Linearity of expectation bounds the number of far candidate occurrences across \(L\) tables by \(L\), whether or not different data points collide independently. With \(L=\lceil p_1^{-k}\rceil=O(n^\rho/p_1)\), independence across tables makes the distinguished near point absent with probability at most \(e^{-1}\). By Markov, the probability of more than \(2L\) far occurrences is at most \(1/2\). On the complement of both bad events, testing \(2L+1\) candidates suffices to return a point within \(cR\). A union bound gives the lecture’s \(1/2-e^{-1}\) success rate. For success at least \(1/2\), use \(L=\lceil p_1^{-k}\log 4\rceil\) and a budget \(4L+1\): each of the two failure probabilities is now at most \(1/4\). Duplicates can count as occurrences in this argument. Any scanned point within \(cR\) already qualifies, whether it is the designated near point or another candidate.

Challenge

Find the behavior of Gaussian bucket hashing when the projected spacing parameter \(w\) is tiny compared with the original distance \(d=\|x-y\|\). Show \(P(h(x)=h(y))\sim w/(d\sqrt{2\pi})\).

Show solution

The density of \(t=d|Z|\) is \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\exp(-t^2/(2d^2))/d\) for \(t\ge0\). Thus

\[P=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\frac wd\int_0^1(1-u)e^{-w^2u^2/(2d^2)}\,du.\]

As \(w/d\to0\), dominated convergence gives an integral tending to \(1/2\), proving the result.

Second challenge. Suppose each query uses \(L\) independent tables from the proved constant-success LSH construction. How many independent copies are required to answer \(Q\) fixed queries with total failure probability at most \(\delta\)?

Show solution to the second challenge

For a query with a near neighbor, each fresh copy fails with probability at most \(1/2\). Trying \(r\) copies independently reduces this to \(2^{-r}\). A union bound over queries gives at most \(Q2^{-r}\) even though different queries reuse the copies. It suffices to take \(r=\lceil\log_2(Q/\delta)\rceil\). This argument assumes the queries are fixed independently of the randomized tables.

Paper challenge · Derive NCA’s gradient and test a collapse

For the NCA objective \(F(A)=\sum_i p_i\), where \(p_i=\sum_{j:y_j=y_i}p_{ij}\), derive the exact matrix gradient. Show that the fully collapsed map \(A=0\) is stationary by parameterization. Then suppose there is a feature direction along which all examples in each class project to their class constant and all class constants are distinct. If there are two or more classes and at least one class has more than one point, prove that collapse is locally improvable. Goldberger et al., “Neighbourhood Components Analysis,” §2

Show paper solution

Let \(D_{ij}=(x_i-x_j)(x_i-x_j)^\top\), \(s_{ij}=-\operatorname{tr}(A D_{ij}A^\top)\), and \(C_i=\{j\ne i:y_j=y_i\}\). Differentiating the softmax gives \(dp_{ij}=p_{ij}(ds_{ij}-\sum_{k\ne i}p_{ik}ds_{ik})\). The score derivative is \(\nabla_A s_{ij}=-2AD_{ij}\). Summing first over \(j\in C_i\), then over \(i\), yields

\[\nabla_A F= 2A\sum_i\left( p_i\sum_{j\ne i}p_{ij}D_{ij}-\sum_{j\in C_i}p_{ij}D_{ij} \right).\]

In particular it vanishes at \(A=0\). This conclusion does not say whether the point optimizes the objective.

Let \(v\) be the stated feature direction and choose \(A=t v^\top\) in one embedding dimension. For anchor \(i\) in class \(c\), the numerator of \(p_i\) is \(|C_i|\) because all its correct neighbors are at zero distance. The denominator is \(|C_i|+\sum_{j:y_j\ne c}\exp(-t^2(\mu_c-\mu_{y_j})^2)\) with distinct class constants \(\mu\). At any nonzero \(t\), each cross-class term strictly decreases relative to \(t=0\). Thus \(p_i\) strictly increases for every anchor with at least one correct neighbor, while the singleton-class probabilities remain zero. Arbitrarily small nonzero \(t\) therefore improves \(F\). The claim in slide 91 relies on a separable perturbation of this kind. With identical features and conflicting labels, such a direction need not exist.

The metric-learning references also discuss learned search embeddings. A spherical embedding can separate relevant pairs while spreading all items across directions to reduce crowding in an index. A triplet margin, a leave-one-out classification score, and an embedding-uniformity term optimize different objectives. Sablayrolles et al., “Spreading Vectors for Similarity Search”
