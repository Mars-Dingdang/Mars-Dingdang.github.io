ML 13: Hyperparameter Optimization and Resource Allocation

Derive Gaussian-process posteriors, Expected Improvement, and hypergradients, then compare resource allocation under successive halving.

The synthetic example picks the unobserved location that maximizes Expected Improvement for a minimization problem.

Training and model selection

The inner procedure produces weights \(w_T(\lambda)\) for hyperparameters \(\lambda\). The outer optimization minimizes \(F(\lambda)=L_{\rm val}(w_T(\lambda),\lambda)\). Fixed training time and full convergence can rank configurations differently. Random seeds make single runs noisy, so comparisons can use common seeds or repeated runs. Keep the final test set separate from the choice of hyperparameters.

A grid with \(m\) values per dimension uses \(m^d\) evaluations. If one coordinate dominates, it tests just \(m\) distinct values for that coordinate. Sampling \(m^d\) configurations from a continuous distribution generally tests a different value each time along that coordinate. Distributions on positive scales that span orders of magnitude are often defined in log space.

Gaussian-process prediction

Let \(F\sim\mathcal{GP}(m,k)\) and observe \(y_j=F(\lambda_j)+e_j\), where independent \(e_j\sim N(0,\sigma_n^2)\). Define \(K_{ij}=k(\lambda_i,\lambda_j)\), \(\Sigma=K+\sigma_n^2I\), and \(k_\star=(k(\lambda_i,\lambda_\star))_i\). Consider the residual

\[r=F(\lambda_\star)-m(\lambda_\star)-k_\star^\top\Sigma^{-1}(y-m_\Lambda).\]

Its covariance with \(y\) is zero. Joint Gaussianity then makes it independent of \(y\). Its variance is obtained by expanding the squared residual. This proves the conditional mean and variance

\[\mu_\star=m(\lambda_\star)+k_\star^\top\Sigma^{-1}(y-m_\Lambda),\qquad s_\star^2=k(\lambda_\star,\lambda_\star)-k_\star^\top\Sigma^{-1}k_\star.\]

To predict a future noisy observation, add \(\sigma_n^2\) to the latent variance.

Expected Improvement

For a reference value \(b\) to beat in minimization, define \(\mathrm{EI}=\mathbb E[(b-F)_+]\). Write \(F=\mu+s Z\) for standard normal \(Z\), and set \(z=(b-\mu)/s\). The integral is

\[\mathrm{EI}=\int_{-\infty}^z(b-\mu-su)\phi(u)\,du =(b-\mu)\Phi(z)+s\phi(z),\]

because \(\phi'(u)=-u\phi(u)\). When \(s=0\), the limit is \((b-\mu)_+\). Low mean and high uncertainty can both make a point worth evaluating. Noisy evaluations may favor basing \(b\) on predicted latent values.

Differentiating through the training trajectory

For learning rate \(\eta\) and \(w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\nabla L_{\rm train}(w_t)\), assume \(w_0\) is independent of \(\eta\). Differentiating both the multiplication by \(\eta\) and the gradient’s input gives

\[u_{t+1}=(I-\eta H_t)u_t-\nabla L_{\rm train}(w_t),\qquad u_t=\partial_\eta w_t,\quad H_t=\nabla^2 L_{\rm train}(w_t),\quad u_0=0.\]

The outer derivative is \(\nabla_wL_{\rm val}(w_T,\eta)^\top u_T+\partial_\eta L_{\rm val}(w_T,\eta)\). A momentum state can be included in the update map and differentiated as another part of the state. With many hyperparameters, reverse-mode differentiation propagates adjoints through the transposed update Jacobians, requiring intermediate states or recomputation. Hessian-vector products suffice.

Successive halving and Hyperband

Start \(n\) candidates with \(r\) units of resource. Rank their scores, retain roughly a fraction \(1/\eta_{\mathrm{sh}}\), and multiply the resource per survivor by \(\eta_{\mathrm{sh}}\). With 16 candidates, resource 1, and elimination factor 2, the round totals from scratch are \(16,16,16,16,16\) epochs. Continuing from checkpoints makes the increments \(16,8,8,8,8\), totaling 48 epochs. The saving assumes earlier training can be reused. Learning curves that cross can eliminate a slow-starting eventual winner. Hyperband distributes its budget among brackets with different initial candidate counts and starting budgets.

In a stationary best-arm model, each configuration \(i\) has reward mean \(\mu_i\). If every arm is sampled \(m\) times with independent rewards in \([0,1]\) and the unique best mean exceeds the others by at least \(\Delta\), Hoeffding bounds the chance of any empirical mean deviating by at least \(\Delta/2\) by \(2n e^{-m\Delta^2/2}\). On the complementary event the empirical best is the genuine best. Early-training scores generally evolve with the allocation, so this stationary guarantee does not transfer automatically.

Challenge

Let the inner objective be \(L_{\rm train}(w,\lambda)=\tfrac12(w-a)^2+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}w^2\) and the outer objective \(\tfrac12(w^\star(\lambda)-b)^2\), with \(a>b>0\) and \(\lambda\ge0\). Derive the exact minimizing regularization and verify the derivative by implicit differentiation.

Show solution

Stationarity is \((1+\lambda)w^\star=a\), so \(w^\star=a/(1+\lambda)\). It decreases from \(a\) to 0, so the squared loss is minimized when \(w^\star=b\), at \(\lambda=a/b-1\). Implicit differentiation of stationarity gives \((1+\lambda)\partial_\lambda w^\star+w^\star=0\). The hypergradient is \(-(w^\star-b)w^\star/(1+\lambda)\), negative below the optimum and positive above it.

Second challenge. Show that Expected Improvement is nondecreasing in predictive standard deviation when the mean and current best are fixed.

Show solution to the second challenge

Write \(a\) for the incumbent minus the mean in minimization, \(z=a/s\), and \(\mathrm{EI}(s)=a\Phi(z)+s\phi(z)\). Differentiate for \(s>0\). The derivatives through \(z\) cancel because \(\phi'(z)=-z\phi(z)\) and \(a=sz\). Hence \(\mathrm{EI}'(s)=\phi(z)>0\). Continuity at \(s=0\) gives \(\max(a,0)\), so uncertainty increases this acquisition value even at fixed predicted mean.
