ML 03: SGD, SVRG, and Mirror Descent

Prove a convergence bound for averaged SGD, derive SVRG’s epoch contraction, and extend descent to the geometry of a probability simplex.

The horizontal axis counts component-gradient evaluations, including the full gradient at each SVRG snapshot. The illustrated snapshots use the last inner iterate.

Which iterate does SGD control?

Let \(f\) be convex and \(L\)-smooth with minimizer \(w^\star\), and set \(w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta G_t\). Conditional on the history \(\mathcal F_t\) before sampling the next direction, assume \(\mathbb E_tG_t=g_t=\nabla f(w_t)\) and \(\mathbb E_t\|G_t-g_t\|^2=v_t\le\sigma^2\). Then

\[\mathbb E_t\|w_{t+1}-w^\star\|^2 =\|w_t-w^\star\|^2-2\eta\langle g_t,w_t-w^\star\rangle+\eta^2(\|g_t\|^2+v_t).\]

Smoothness gives \(\mathbb E_t f(w_{t+1})\le f(w_t)-\eta\|g_t\|^2+(L\eta^2/2)(\|g_t\|^2+v_t)\). Combine this with convexity and use \(\eta\le1/L\):

\[\begin{aligned} \mathbb E_t[f(w_{t+1})-f^\star] &\le \langle g_t,w_t-w^\star\rangle-\tfrac\eta2\|g_t\|^2+\tfrac\eta2v_t\\ &=\frac{\|w_t-w^\star\|^2-\mathbb E_t\|w_{t+1}-w^\star\|^2}{2\eta}+\eta v_t. \end{aligned}\]

Take total expectations, sum over \(T\) steps, and apply Jensen’s inequality at \(\bar w_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T w_t\):

\[\mathbb E[f(\bar w_T)-f^\star]\le \frac{\|w_0-w^\star\|^2}{2\eta T}+\eta\sigma^2.\]

A step proportional to \(T^{-1/2}\) balances the terms. A uniform random output iterate has the same objective bound. The proof does not imply a bound for the last iterate because stochastic losses need not decrease monotonically. An average of \(b\) conditionally independent sample gradients has variance \(\sigma^2/b\) when the individual variance is bounded by \(\sigma^2\).

\(1/T\) stochastic rate under strong convexity">The \(1/T\) stochastic rate under strong convexity

For a \(\mu\)-strongly convex and \(L\)-smooth \(f\) on a closed convex set containing an unconstrained minimizer \(w^\star\), use the update with Euclidean projection and assume \(\mathbb E_t\|G_t\|^2\le G^2\) along the trajectory. Projection cannot increase the distance to a feasible comparator: the projection’s first-order condition makes the cross term in the squared-distance expansion nonnegative. Strong monotonicity gives \(\langle\nabla f(w_t),w_t-w^\star\rangle\ge\mu\|w_t-w^\star\|^2\). Therefore for \(\eta_t=1/(\mu(t+1))\) and \(t\ge1\),

\[\mathbb E\|w_{t+1}-w^\star\|^2 \le\frac{t-1}{t+1}\mathbb E\|w_t-w^\star\|^2+\frac{G^2}{\mu^2(t+1)^2}.\]

Let \(C=\max\{\mathbb E\|w_1-w^\star\|^2,G^2/\mu^2\}\). If the distance bound is \(C/t\), the right side is at most \(C/(t+1)\) because the remaining slack before the noise term is \(C/(t(t+1))\). Induction gives squared distance \(O(1/t)\). The smooth upper model at \(w^\star\) has zero linear term, so the expected last-iterate objective gap is at most \(LC/(2t)\). This gives the stochastic rate contrasted with geometric decay for deterministic strongly convex GD in the 2026 slide 10 comparison.

SVRG as a control variate

Take \(f=n^{-1}\sum_i f_i\), where each \(f_i\) is convex and \(L\)-smooth. At an epoch snapshot \(\tilde w\), compute its full gradient. Sample \(i\) uniformly and use

\[V_i=\nabla f_i(w)-\nabla f_i(\tilde w)+\nabla f(\tilde w).\]

Its conditional expectation is \(\nabla f(w)\). If \(A_i=\nabla f_i(w)-\nabla f_i(\tilde w)\), its variance is \(\mathbb E\|A_i-\mathbb EA_i\|^2\le\mathbb E\|A_i\|^2\le L^2\|w-\tilde w\|^2\). The same index in both evaluations makes this cancellation possible.

For an objective-gap bound, define \(a_i(u)=\nabla f_i(u)-\nabla f_i(w^\star)\). The function \(h_i(u)=f_i(u)-\nabla f_i(w^\star)^\top u\) is minimized at \(w^\star\). A \(1/L\) gradient step on \(h_i\) cannot improve more than its current gap, so

\[\|a_i(u)\|^2\le 2L[f_i(u)-f_i(w^\star)-\langle\nabla f_i(w^\star),u-w^\star\rangle].\]

Averaging gives \(\mathbb E_i\|a_i(u)\|^2\le2L\Delta(u)\), where \(\Delta(u)=f(u)-f^\star\). Write \(V_i=a_i(w)-(a_i(\tilde w)-\mathbb Ea_i(\tilde w))\) and bound the squared sum by twice each squared term. It follows that

\[\mathbb E_i\|V_i\|^2\le4L(\Delta(w)+\Delta(\tilde w)).\]

By expansion of the next squared distance and convexity,

\[\mathbb E_i\|w^+-w^\star\|^2+2\eta(1-2L\eta)\Delta(w) \le\|w-w^\star\|^2+4L\eta^2\Delta(\tilde w).\]

If \(f\) is \(\mu\)-strongly convex, start \(w_0=\tilde w\), sum this bound over \(m\) updates, and choose \(\tilde w^+\) uniformly from \(w_0,\ldots,w_{m-1}\). The starting squared distance is at most \(2\Delta(\tilde w)/\mu\), so

\[\mathbb E[\Delta(\tilde w^+)\mid\tilde w] \le \frac{1/(m\mu\eta)+2L\eta}{1-2L\eta}\Delta(\tilde w).\]

Choosing \(\eta=1/(8L)\) and \(m\ge32L/\mu\) gives a factor at most \(2/3\). An epoch costs \(n+2m\) component evaluations. To reduce the initial gap by a factor \(\varepsilon\), this takes \(O((n+L/\mu)\log(1/\varepsilon))\) component-gradient evaluations. Full gradient descent costs \(O(n(L/\mu)\log(1/\varepsilon))\) evaluations by the rate in Lecture 2.

For a composite objective \(P=f+R\), with a proper convex regularizer \(R\), the optimality condition \(-\nabla f(w^\star)\in\partial R(w^\star)\) implies \(\Delta_P(u)\ge\Delta_f(u)-\langle\nabla f(w^\star),u-w^\star\rangle\). The same argument therefore bounds the second moment of \(V_i-\nabla f(w^\star)\) by \(4L(\Delta_P(w)+\Delta_P(\tilde w))\).

Mirror descent and the simplex

A convex function’s tangent at each queried point \(w_t\) gives a lower bound on \(f(u)\). Average those inequalities and use Jensen at \(\bar w_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}w_t\) to obtain

\[f(\bar w_T)-f(u)\le T^{-1}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\langle\nabla f(w_t),w_t-u\rangle.\]

Thus controlling the sum of the linearized gaps also controls the objective value of the averaged iterate.

A differentiable convex potential \(\psi\) defines the Bregman divergence

\[D_\psi(u,w)=\psi(u)-\psi(w)-\langle\nabla\psi(w),u-w\rangle.\]

The update minimizes \(\eta\langle g_t,u\rangle+D_\psi(u,w_t)\) on a convex set. In the interior of an unconstrained domain the equation is \(\nabla\psi(w_{t+1})=\nabla\psi(w_t)-\eta g_t\). A squared-Euclidean potential gives projected gradient descent.

The three-point identity follows by expanding the three divergences and canceling potential values:

\[D_\psi(u,w_t)-D_\psi(u,w_{t+1})-D_\psi(w_{t+1},w_t) =\langle\nabla\psi(w_{t+1})-\nabla\psi(w_t),u-w_{t+1}\rangle.\]

The right side is the change in the tangent model evaluated at \(u\), after accounting for the old tangent’s gap at \(w_{t+1}\). The variational inequality for the mirror update says that adding \(\eta\langle g_t,u-w_{t+1}\rangle\) to this inner product makes it nonnegative. Thus

\[\eta\langle g_t,w_{t+1}-u\rangle\le D_\psi(u,w_t)-D_\psi(u,w_{t+1})-D_\psi(w_{t+1},w_t).\]

For a 1-strongly convex potential, adding \(\eta\langle g_t,w_t-w_{t+1}\rangle\) and applying Young’s inequality in the primal and dual norms cancels the last divergence. Sum over \(t\) to get

\[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\langle g_t,w_t-u\rangle \le D_\psi(u,w_0)/\eta+\tfrac\eta2\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\|g_t\|_\ast^2.\]

For \(\|g_t\|_\ast\le\rho\) and \(\Theta=D_\psi(u,w_0)>0\), choose \(\eta=\sqrt{2\Theta}/(\rho\sqrt T)\) for average regret at most \(\rho\sqrt{2\Theta/T}\). With \(g_t=\nabla f(w_t)\), the same bound holds for the objective gap of the averaged iterate.

Legendre coordinates and projection

For a differentiable Legendre potential, write \(p=\nabla\psi(x)\) and \(\psi^\ast(p)=\sup_v(\langle p,v\rangle-\psi(v))\). At the interior optimizer the stationarity condition is \(\nabla\psi(v)=p\), and the derivative of the conjugate is \(\nabla\psi^\ast(p)=v\). Fenchel equality \(\psi^\ast(p)=\langle p,x\rangle-\psi(x)\) yields

\[D_\psi(u,x)=\psi(u)+\psi^\ast(p)-\langle p,u\rangle =D_{\psi^\ast}(\nabla\psi(x),\nabla\psi(u)).\]

The last equality follows by inserting \(\nabla\psi^\ast(\nabla\psi(u))=u\) and applying Fenchel equality at \(u\). In dual coordinates the divergence reverses its arguments. If \(y=\arg\min_{v\in C}D_\psi(v,x)\) for convex \(C\), its first-order condition is \(\langle\nabla\psi(y)-\nabla\psi(x),u-y\rangle\ge0\) for \(u\in C\). The three-point identity then gives \(D_\psi(u,x)\ge D_\psi(u,y)+D_\psi(y,x)\). Equality holds when the primal chord \(u-y\) is orthogonal to the dual chord \(\nabla\psi(y)-\nabla\psi(x)\). Amari, Information Geometry and Its Applications, §§1.3–1.4 and 1.6.

Negative entropy and multiplicative weights

On the probability simplex, use \(\psi(w)=\sum_j w_j\log w_j\). With positive initial coordinates, the gradient components are \(1+\log w_j\). The equality constraint introduces a common additive term, removed by normalization:

\[w_{t+1,j}=\frac{w_{t,j}e^{-\eta g_{t,j}}}{\sum_k w_{t,k}e^{-\eta g_{t,k}}}.\]

On the simplex interior the Hessian is diagonal with entries \(1/w_j\). For a displacement \(v\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \((\sum_j|v_j|)^2\le(\sum_jw_j)(\sum_jv_j^2/w_j)=v^\top\nabla^2\psi(w)v\). Integrating along a segment yields \(\ell_1\) strong convexity, extended by continuity to boundary targets. The dual norm is \(\ell_\infty\). For uniform \(w_0\), \(D_\psi(u,w_0)\le\log d\). If \(\|g_t\|_\infty\le\rho\), optimizing the step in the regret bound gives average regret at most \(\rho\sqrt{2\log d/T}\). With Euclidean projected descent the uniform-start squared diameter is at most 1, but gradients can have \(\ell_2\) norm \(\rho\sqrt d\), giving average regret at most \(\rho\sqrt{d/T}\). The corresponding iteration bounds at accuracy \(\varepsilon\) scale as \(\rho^2\log d/\varepsilon^2\) and \(\rho^2d/\varepsilon^2\).

Mirror descent and cumulative linearization

In the unconstrained interior setting, initialize at a differentiable minimizer \(w_0\) of \(\psi\), so \(\nabla\psi(w_0)=0\). Unrolling the dual update gives \(\nabla\psi(w_{t+1})=-\eta\sum_{j=0}^t g_j\). This is also the optimality equation for minimizing \(\psi(u)+\eta\langle\sum_{j=0}^t g_j,u\rangle\). Strict convexity makes each minimizer unique. Induction from the common initialization therefore makes the two trajectories identical under these hypotheses.

Projection adds a normal-cone term. For example, take \([-1,1]\), \(\psi(w)=w^2/2\), step 1, and \(f(w)=3w^2/2+2w\). Both methods start at zero and have \(w_1=-1\). The next gradient is \(-1\), so projected descent moves to \(0\). The cumulative linearized objective instead minimizes \(u^2/2+(2-1)u\) over the interval and stays at \(-1\).

Challenges

Problem 1

Let \(a_i\) be a stored gradient for component \(i\) before the update and let \(\bar a=n^{-1}\sum_i a_i\). Derive the conditional means of SAG’s direction \(\bar a+(\nabla f_i(w)-a_i)/n\) and SAGA’s direction \(\bar a+\nabla f_i(w)-a_i\). Give a state where the first direction vanishes while the true gradient is nonzero.

Show solution

Under a uniform fresh choice of \(i\), the mean of the replacement difference is \(\nabla f(w)-\bar a\). The SAG mean is \((1-1/n)\bar a+\nabla f(w)/n\) and the SAGA mean is \(\nabla f(w)\). For \(n=2\) let both components equal \(u^2/2\), set \(w=1\), and let both stored gradients equal \(-1\), as obtained at \(u=-1\). The SAG direction after one replacement is zero for either sampled component, while the full gradient is 1. The example concerns the estimator conditional on the stored state.

Problem 2

Build unbiased SVRG for nonuniform probabilities \(p_i>0\). If component \(i\) is \(L_i\)-smooth, choose the probabilities to minimize the bound based only on these positive constants.

Show solution

Set \(a_i=\nabla f_i(w)-\nabla f_i(\tilde w)\) and use \(a_i/(np_i)+\nabla f(\tilde w)\). Its variance is at most \(n^{-2}\sum_i\|a_i\|^2/p_i\le n^{-2}\|w-\tilde w\|^2\sum_iL_i^2/p_i\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\sum_i L_i^2/p_i\ge(\sum_iL_i)^2\) with equality when \(p_i=L_i/\sum_jL_j\).

Problem 3. Accelerate gradient descent by coupling two updates

Let \(f\) be convex and \(L\)-smooth on a convex set \(Q\) in a norm \(\|\cdot\|\), and let \(\psi\) be a differentiable 1-strongly convex potential in that norm. Start \(y_0=z_0\). At step \(k\ge0\) set \(a=(k+2)/(2L)\), \(\tau=1/(aL)\), and \(x=(1-\tau)y_k+\tau z_k\). For \(g=\nabla f(x)\), use

\[y_{k+1}=\arg\min_{y\in Q}\{\langle g,y-x\rangle+\tfrac L2\|y-x\|^2\},\qquad z_{k+1}=\arg\min_{z\in Q}\{a\langle g,z-z_k\rangle+D_\psi(z,z_k)\}.\]

Assume the subproblems and a minimizer \(u^\star\) exist. Prove \(f(y_T)-f(u^\star)\le4LD_\psi(u^\star,z_0)/(T+1)^2\) for \(T\ge1\). Allen-Zhu and Orecchia, Linear Coupling, Theorem 4.1, Lemmas 4.2–4.3 and Appendix C.

Show solution

At step \(k\), let \(G=f(x)-f(y_{k+1})\) and \(z^+=z_{k+1}\). The mirror variational inequality and strong convexity give for any \(u\in Q\)

\[a\langle g,z_k-u\rangle \le a\langle g,z_k-z^+\rangle-\tfrac12\|z_k-z^+\|^2+D_\psi(u,z_k)-D_\psi(u,z^+).\]

Set \(v=(1-\tau)y_k+\tau z^+\in Q\), so \(x-v=\tau(z_k-z^+)\). The first two terms become

\[a^2L\big(\langle g,x-v\rangle-\tfrac L2\|x-v\|^2\big)\le a^2LG.\]

The bracket is the model decrease at \(v\). The gradient update maximizes that decrease, and smoothness guarantees an actual decrease at least as large as the achieved model decrease.

By convexity, \(a(f(x)-f(u))\le a\langle g,x-u\rangle\). Split its right side at \(z_k\) and substitute \(x-z_k=((1-\tau)/\tau)(y_k-x)\). The coefficient \((1-\tau)a/\tau=a^2L-a\) is nonnegative, so convexity at \(y_k\) and the bound for the mirror step give

\[a^2L(f(y_{k+1})-f(u)) \le(a^2L-a)(f(y_k)-f(u))+D_\psi(u,z_k)-D_\psi(u,z_{k+1}).\]

For \(u=u^\star\) all objective gaps are nonnegative. At \(k=0\), \(a^2L-a=0\). For \(k\ge1\), \(a^2L-a=k(k+2)/(4L)\le(k+1)^2/(4L)\), the preceding step’s coefficient on \(f(y_k)-f(u^\star)\). The potential consisting of the weighted objective gap plus \(D_\psi(u^\star,z_k)\) therefore never increases. After \(T\) steps the coefficient is \((T+1)^2/(4L)\). Drop the nonnegative final divergence and divide.
