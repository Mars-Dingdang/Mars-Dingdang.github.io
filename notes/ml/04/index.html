ML 04: Matrix Completion and Nonconvex Geometry

Derive the nuclear norm factorization, alternating least squares, and a strict-saddle example, then identify the information required for completion.

A rank-one fit recovers the hidden values in this connected synthetic observation pattern.

The observation operator

A rating matrix \(M\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\) may have entries \(u_i^\top v_j\), where user and item vectors have dimension \(r\). This implies rank at most \(r\). The operator \(P_\Omega\) preserves observed entries and sets other entries to zero. Exact low-rank completion asks for \(\min_X\operatorname{rank}(X)\) subject to \(P_\Omega X=P_\Omega M\). Unobserved values are free variables. Fitting ordinary low-rank approximation to a zero-filled matrix includes artificial zero ratings in its objective.

Low rank and identifiability

For an unobserved row of a rank-one matrix \(uv^\top\), the corresponding coordinate of \(u\) is undetermined. For \(M=e_ie_j^\top\), missing just entry \((i,j)\) leaves its scale undetermined. A recovery assumption should address both insufficient sampling and concentration of singular vectors.

For a thin SVD \(M=U\Sigma V^\top\), typical incoherence conditions are

\[\max_i\|U^\top e_i\|^2\le\mu r/m,\qquad \max_j\|V^\top e_j\|^2\le\mu r/n .\]

The first quantities are diagonal entries of the orthogonal projector \(UU^\top\). They sum to \(\operatorname{tr}(UU^\top)=r\), so \(\mu\ge1\). A uniform unit singular vector attains \(\mu=1\), whereas a coordinate vector in \(\mathbb R^m\) needs \(\mu=m\). For a full-column-rank basis \(B\), calculate the same scores from \(\operatorname{diag}(B(B^\top B)^{-1}B^\top)\). Recovery results additionally depend on the sample size and selection process.

From singular values to a convex objective

The nuclear norm \(\|X\|_\ast=\sum_i\sigma_i(X)\) is the dual of the spectral norm: writing \(X=\sum_i\sigma_i u_iv_i^\top\) gives \(\langle Z,X\rangle\le\|Z\|_{\rm op}\sum_i\sigma_i\). Equality is achieved at \(Z=UV^\top\) with \(\|Z\|_{\rm op}=1\) for nonzero \(X\). As a supremum of linear functions it is convex. Moreover, for factor widths at least \(\operatorname{rank}(X)\),

\[\|X\|_\ast=\min_{AB^\top=X}\tfrac12(\|A\|_F^2+\|B\|_F^2).\]

To attain the bound take \(A=U\Sigma^{1/2}\) and \(B=V\Sigma^{1/2}\). For the other direction, any \(\|Z\|_{\rm op}\le1\) satisfies \(\langle Z,AB^\top\rangle=\langle Z^\top A,B\rangle\le\|A\|_F\|B\|_F\le(\|A\|_F^2+\|B\|_F^2)/2\). Maximize over \(Z\). The identity connects convex nuclear-norm penalization to Frobenius-regularized factor fitting, where fixing a narrower factor width imposes an additional rank constraint.

Alternating minimization and strict saddles

For

\[F(A,B)=\tfrac12\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega}(a_i^\top b_j-M_{ij})^2 +\tfrac\lambda2(\|A\|_F^2+\|B\|_F^2),\]

holding \(B\) fixed gives independent row subproblems, with normal equation

\[\left(\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\Omega}b_jb_j^\top+\lambda I\right)a_i =\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\Omega}M_{ij}b_j.\]

The update for \(B\) interchanges the roles. Exact subproblem solutions never increase \(F\). For the product alone, \((A,B)\mapsto(AR,BR^{-\top})\) preserves every prediction for invertible \(R\). The Frobenius regularizer preserves this symmetry for orthogonal \(R\).

For \(F(a,b)=\tfrac12(ab-1)^2\), the gradient vanishes at \((0,0)\). The Hessian there is

\[\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\]

with eigenvalues \(\pm1\). Taylor expansion along \((1,1)\) gives local decrease and expansion along \((1,-1)\) gives local increase. The functions \(t^4,-t^4,t^3\) all have first and second derivatives zero at the origin, yet give a local minimum, a local maximum, and neither. Negative curvature supplies information that flat second-order stationarity lacks.

The three regions in a strict-saddle analysis

With an unbiased perturbation \(\xi\) of conditional second moment at most \(\sigma^2\), the \(L\)-smooth update \(w^+=w-\eta(\nabla f(w)+\xi)\) gives

\[\mathbb E[f(w^+)-f(w)\mid w]\le -\eta(1-L\eta/2)\|\nabla f(w)\|^2+\tfrac{L\eta^2}{2}\sigma^2.\]

This follows by expanding the upper quadratic bound and dropping the cross term in expectation. A sufficiently large gradient produces a quantitative decrease.

In the negative-curvature region, suppose \(H=\nabla^2 f(w)\) has a unit eigenvector \(v\) of eigenvalue \(-\gamma<0\). In the quadratic model about \(w\), iteration has linear part \(I-\eta H\) and amplifies a displacement along \(v\) by \(1+\eta\gamma\). A perturbation with enough mass along \(v\) can seed this displacement. For an \(M\)-Lipschitz Hessian, subtracting the linear gradient expansion leaves a remainder bounded by \(M\|z\|^2/2\) at displacement \(z\), by integrating the Hessian difference along the segment. This controls the approximation over a small neighborhood. In particular, a direct signed step of radius \(r\) along \(v\), choosing the sign for a nonpositive linear term, gives \(f(w\pm rv)-f(w)\le-\gamma r^2/2+Mr^3/6\).

Near a local minimum, favorable curvature and sufficiently small steps and perturbations help keep iterates inside its basin. If the conditional probability of exiting in a step is bounded by \(p\) while still inside, the chance of any exit within \(T\) steps is at most \(Tp\) by a union bound on the first exit. The course’s informal strict-saddle guarantee combines these three regions: outside the basin there must be quantitative decrease over one or more steps, and a lower bound on the objective limits the number of such stages. Recovery additionally depends on identifiability. A global minimizer of the observed objective can still fail to recover \(M\).

Challenges

Problem 1

Suppose every observed value of a nonzero rank-one matrix is nonzero. Give necessary and sufficient graph conditions for unique rank-one completion among real matrices, including vertices with no observed edges.

Show solution

View rows and columns as vertices of a bipartite graph with observed entries as edges. If the graph is connected, fix one nonzero factor coordinate as a gauge and propagate each adjacent factor by dividing the edge value by the known coordinate. Existence of the given completion ensures consistency around cycles. All factors are then determined up to one global rescaling and every product is fixed. If an isolated row or column is present, its factor can vary while preserving the observations and changing at least one unobserved product. If two components contain edges, independently rescale the row factors in one by \(c\) and the column factors there by \(1/c\). Cross-component entries change. In the no-observation case a real rank-one completion is plainly nonunique.

Problem 2

A \(4\times4\) matrix has its first two rows equal to \((9,0,1,0)\) and the other two equal to \((2,2,2,2)\). Compute its smallest joint incoherence constant without an SVD.

Show solution

The column projector has constant diagonal \(1/2\). For the row projector, form \(B\) from the distinct rows and use \(B^\top(BB^\top)^{-1}B\). The inner inverse is \(\frac1{912}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}16&-20\\-20&82\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). The four column quadratic forms give the diagonal \((113,41,33,41)/114\). The rank is 2, so the joint constant is \((4/2)\max\{1/2,113/114\}=113/57\).
