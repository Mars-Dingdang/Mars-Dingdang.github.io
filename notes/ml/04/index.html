ML 04: Matrix Completion and Nonconvex Geometry

Derive the nuclear norm factorization, alternating least squares, and a strict-saddle example, then identify the information required for completion.

A rank-one fit recovers the hidden values in this connected synthetic observation pattern.

The observation operator

A rating matrix \(M\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\) may have entries \(u_i^\top v_j\), where user and item vectors have dimension \(r\). This implies rank at most \(r\). The operator \(P_\Omega\) preserves observed entries and sets other entries to zero. Exact low-rank completion asks for \(\min_X\operatorname{rank}(X)\) subject to \(P_\Omega X=P_\Omega M\). Unobserved values are free variables. Fitting ordinary low-rank approximation to a zero-filled matrix includes artificial zero ratings in its objective.

Low rank and identifiability

For an unobserved row of a rank-one matrix \(uv^\top\), the corresponding coordinate of \(u\) is undetermined. For \(M=e_ie_j^\top\), missing just entry \((i,j)\) leaves its scale undetermined. A recovery assumption should address both insufficient sampling and concentration of singular vectors.

For a thin SVD \(M=U\Sigma V^\top\), typical incoherence conditions are

\[\max_i\|U^\top e_i\|^2\le\mu r/m,\qquad \max_j\|V^\top e_j\|^2\le\mu r/n .\]

The first quantities are diagonal entries of the orthogonal projector \(UU^\top\). They sum to \(\operatorname{tr}(UU^\top)=r\), so \(\mu\ge1\). A uniform unit singular vector attains \(\mu=1\), whereas a coordinate vector in \(\mathbb R^m\) needs \(\mu=m\). For a full-column-rank basis \(B\), calculate the same scores from \(\operatorname{diag}(B(B^\top B)^{-1}B^\top)\). Recovery results additionally depend on the sample size and selection process.

For the factor incoherence used in PSD completion, write \(M=ZZ^\top\), let \(U\) span its range, and denote the condition number of \(Z\) by \(\kappa\). Since the squared norm of row \(Z_i\) is \(M_{ii}\),

\[\sigma_{\min}(Z)^2\|U^\top e_i\|^2\le \|Z_i\|^2 \le\sigma_{\max}(Z)^2\|U^\top e_i\|^2 .\]

Thus the leverage-score and factor-row conditions express related restrictions, with constants depending on the singular values. For instance, \(\|U^\top e_i\|^2\le\mu_0 r/d\) implies \(\|Z_i\|\le\kappa\sqrt{\mu_0/d}\|Z\|_F\) because \(\|Z\|_F^2\ge r\sigma_{\min}(Z)^2\). Conversely \(\|Z_i\|\le(\mu/\sqrt d)\|Z\|_F\) bounds the leverage by \(\mu^2\kappa^2r/d\). A uniformly sampled observation set still needs enough entries to cover rows and columns. Under independent entry sampling at rate \(p\), a given row of an \(m\) by \(n\) matrix is unobserved with probability \((1-p)^n\), so a union bound for any empty row is \(m(1-p)^n\).

From singular values to a convex objective

The nuclear norm \(\|X\|_\ast=\sum_i\sigma_i(X)\) is the dual of the spectral norm: writing \(X=\sum_i\sigma_i u_iv_i^\top\) gives \(\langle Z,X\rangle\le\|Z\|_{\rm op}\sum_i\sigma_i\). Equality is achieved at \(Z=UV^\top\) with \(\|Z\|_{\rm op}=1\) for nonzero \(X\). As a supremum of linear functions it is convex. Moreover, for factor widths at least \(\operatorname{rank}(X)\),

\[\|X\|_\ast=\min_{AB^\top=X}\tfrac12(\|A\|_F^2+\|B\|_F^2).\]

To attain the bound take \(A=U\Sigma^{1/2}\) and \(B=V\Sigma^{1/2}\). For the other direction, any \(\|Z\|_{\rm op}\le1\) satisfies \(\langle Z,AB^\top\rangle=\langle Z^\top A,B\rangle\le\|A\|_F\|B\|_F\le(\|A\|_F^2+\|B\|_F^2)/2\). Maximize over \(Z\). The identity connects convex nuclear-norm penalization to Frobenius-regularized factor fitting, where fixing a narrower factor width imposes an additional rank constraint.

A semidefinite formulation

Introduce symmetric \(R,S\) and constrain \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}R&X\\X^\top&S\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\succeq0\). Any such matrix admits a Gram factorization \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A\\B\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A\\B\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)^\top\), so \(X=AB^\top\) and \((\operatorname{tr}R+\operatorname{tr}S)/2=(\|A\|_F^2+\|B\|_F^2)/2\ge\|X\|_\ast\). Conversely, the balanced factors from the SVD attain equality. Minimizing this linear trace objective subject to the block constraint and the observed entries therefore gives precisely nuclear norm completion. The block matrix explains why a general semidefinite implementation uses substantially more storage than the factor representation.

Alternating minimization and strict saddles

For

\[F(A,B)=\tfrac12\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega}(a_i^\top b_j-M_{ij})^2 +\tfrac\lambda2(\|A\|_F^2+\|B\|_F^2),\]

holding \(B\) fixed gives independent row subproblems, with normal equation

\[\left(\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\Omega}b_jb_j^\top+\lambda I\right)a_i =\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\Omega}M_{ij}b_j.\]

The update for \(B\) interchanges the roles. Exact subproblem solutions never increase \(F\). For the product alone, \((A,B)\mapsto(AR,BR^{-\top})\) preserves every prediction for invertible \(R\). The Frobenius regularizer preserves this symmetry for orthogonal \(R\).

The unregularized observed-factor loss is not jointly convex, even for one nonzero observed entry. With target \(1\), the factor pairs \((1,1)\) and \((3,1/3)\) both have zero loss, but their average \((2,2/3)\) predicts \(4/3\) and has positive loss. A nonconvex factor loss can have many representations of a global solution while still having no spurious local minima. Those two properties are compatible.

For \(F(a,b)=\tfrac12(ab-1)^2\), the gradient vanishes at \((0,0)\). The Hessian there is

\[\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\]

with eigenvalues \(\pm1\). Taylor expansion along \((1,1)\) gives local decrease and expansion along \((1,-1)\) gives local increase. The functions \(t^4,-t^4,t^3\) all have first and second derivatives zero at the origin, yet give a local minimum, a local maximum, and neither. Negative curvature supplies information that flat second-order stationarity lacks.

The three regions in a strict-saddle analysis

With an unbiased perturbation \(\xi\) of conditional second moment at most \(\sigma^2\), the \(L\)-smooth update \(w^+=w-\eta(\nabla f(w)+\xi)\) gives

\[\mathbb E[f(w^+)-f(w)\mid w]\le -\eta(1-L\eta/2)\|\nabla f(w)\|^2+\tfrac{L\eta^2}{2}\sigma^2.\]

This follows by expanding the upper quadratic bound and dropping the cross term in expectation. A sufficiently large gradient produces a quantitative decrease.

In the negative-curvature region, suppose \(H=\nabla^2 f(w)\) has a unit eigenvector \(v\) of eigenvalue \(-\gamma<0\). In the quadratic model about \(w\), iteration has linear part \(I-\eta H\) and amplifies a displacement along \(v\) by \(1+\eta\gamma\). A perturbation with enough mass along \(v\) can seed this displacement. For an \(M\)-Lipschitz Hessian, subtracting the linear gradient expansion leaves a remainder bounded by \(M\|z\|^2/2\) at displacement \(z\), by integrating the Hessian difference along the segment. This controls the approximation over a small neighborhood. In particular, a direct signed step of radius \(r\) along \(v\), choosing the sign for a nonpositive linear term, gives \(f(w\pm rv)-f(w)\le-\gamma r^2/2+Mr^3/6\).

Near a local minimum, favorable curvature and sufficiently small steps and perturbations help keep iterates inside its basin. If the conditional probability of exiting in a step is bounded by \(p\) while still inside, the chance of any exit within \(T\) steps is at most \(Tp\) by a union bound on the first exit. The course’s informal strict-saddle guarantee combines these three regions: outside the basin there must be quantitative decrease over one or more steps, and a lower bound on the objective limits the number of such stages. Recovery additionally depends on identifiability. A global minimizer of the observed objective can still fail to recover \(M\).

From linearized escape to the noisy-descent theorem

The quantitative hypothesis in Ge et al. asks that at every point there be a gradient of norm at least \(\epsilon\), curvature at most \(-\gamma\), or a local minimum within distance \(\delta\) with an \(\alpha\)-strongly convex neighborhood of twice that radius. The theorem also assumes a bounded objective, a Lipschitz gradient and Hessian, and bounded stochastic noise, with added noise providing variance along every direction. For sufficiently small constant step \(\eta\) and failure probability \(\zeta\), it gives a terminal iterate within \(\widetilde O(\sqrt{\eta\log(1/(\eta\zeta))})\) of a local minimum after \(\widetilde O(\eta^{-2}\log(1/\zeta))\) steps. The analysis holds up to fixed polynomial dependencies on the problem parameters.

Here are the details of the unconstrained argument for additive martingale-difference noise with bounded norm and conditional covariance \(\nu^2 I\), the isotropic setting of Appendix A. Treat dimension and the geometric and noise constants as fixed in the step-size estimates. Write \(h=\log(1/\eta)\) and take \(0<\eta\) sufficiently small.

Large gradient. When \(\eta\le1/L\), the smoothness inequality above is at most \(-\eta\|\nabla f(w)\|^2/2+L\eta^2d\nu^2/2\). For \(\|\nabla f(w)\|^2\ge2L\eta d\nu^2\) this is at most \(-Ld\nu^2\eta^2/2\). The gradient cutoff is therefore \(O(\sqrt\eta)\), below the fixed \(\epsilon\) in the strict-saddle condition. If the gradient is below the cutoff and there is no sufficiently negative eigenvalue, the condition places the point in a strongly convex neighborhood. The lower curvature there, together with \(\nabla f(w^\star)=0\), gives \(\|\nabla f(w)\|\ge\alpha\|w-w^\star\|\). Entry occurs within \(O(\sqrt\eta)\) of the local minimum.

Escape. At the initial point \(w_0\) of an escape window, let \(g_0=\nabla f(w_0)\), \(H=\nabla^2 f(w_0)\), \(\lambda_{\min}(H)=-\gamma_0\le-\gamma\), and \(A=I-\eta H\). In the quadratic model \(\tilde f(w_0+x)=f(w_0)+g_0^\top x+x^\top Hx/2\), let \(\tilde x_t\) be the displacement after \(t\) noisy steps with the same errors as the actual sequence. Unrolling the linear recurrence gives

\[\tilde x_t=-\eta\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}A^jg_0-\eta\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}A^{t-1-j}\xi_j .\]

Choose the first \(T\) for which \(\gamma_0\sum_{j<T}(1+\eta\gamma_0)^{2j}\ge d/\eta\). The geometric sum gives \(T=O(\log(d+1)/(\eta\gamma_0))\) and \((1+\eta\gamma_0)^T=O(\sqrt{d+1})\). The mean iterate follows deterministic gradient descent on the quadratic, so its objective is at most the initial one by its \(L\)-smoothness. The centered displacement has covariance \(\eta^2\nu^2\sum_{j<T}A^{2j}\). Cross terms between noises at different times have conditional expectation zero. Thus the expected model objective change is at most

\[\frac{\eta^2\nu^2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^d\lambda_i(H)\sum_{j<T}(1-\eta\lambda_i(H))^{2j}.\]

For \(\lambda_i>0\) the inner weighted sum is bounded by \(\lambda_i/(1-(1-\eta\lambda_i)^2)\le1/\eta\). One negative eigenvalue contributes at most \(-d/\eta\) by construction, and the others can only lower the result. The model drop is at least \(\eta\nu^2/2\) in expectation.

For a fixed coordinate and \(t\le T\), the centered displacement is a sum of bounded martingale differences with total squared increment bounds \(O(\eta^2\sum_{j<t}\|A^j\|^2)=O(\eta)\), since \(\|A^j\|\le(1+\eta\gamma_0)^j\). Apply Azuma–Hoeffding and union bound over coordinates and times to get \(\max_{t\le T}\|\tilde x_t\|\le C\sqrt\eta\,h\) with probability at least \(1-O(\eta^3)\), including the deterministic mean, whose norm is at most \(\eta\sum_{j<T}\|A^j\|\|g_0\|=O(\sqrt\eta)\).

For the real displacement \(x_t=w_t-w_0\), the gradient remainder is \(R(x_t)=\nabla f(w_0+x_t)-g_0-Hx_t\), with \(\|R(x_t)\|\le M\|x_t\|^2/2\). Because both sequences receive the same noise, their error is exactly

\[x_t-\tilde x_t=-\eta\sum_{j<t}A^{t-1-j}R(x_j).\]

If all earlier \(\|x_j\|\le2C\sqrt\eta\,h\), this sum has norm at most a constant times \(\eta h^2\) because \(\eta\sum_{j<T}\|A^j\|=O(1)\). For sufficiently small \(\eta\), the bound is smaller than \(C\sqrt\eta\,h\), closing the induction. Taylor’s theorem for function values bounds \(f(w_T)-\tilde f(w_0+\tilde x_T)\) by \(O(\eta^{3/2}h^3)\) on the event: the linear-in-error term multiplies the error \(O(\eta h^2)\) by the model gradient \(g_0+H\tilde x_T=O(\sqrt\eta\,h)\), and the squared-error and cubic Taylor terms have the same or smaller order. Off the event, the bounded true objective and the uniform bound \(\|\tilde x_t\|=O(1)\) from the unrolled model give an \(O(\eta^3)\) contribution. Combining with the model drop gives \(\mathbb E[f(w_T)-f(w_0)]\le-c\eta\) for some \(c>0\).

Residence near a minimum. Let \(V_t=\|w_t-w^\star\|^2\) and \(a=1-\eta\alpha\). Inside the strongly convex region, use strong gradient monotonicity and \(\|\nabla f(w_t)\|\le L\sqrt{V_t}\). For \(\eta L^2\le\alpha\), conditional expectation satisfies \(\mathbb E_t V_{t+1}\le aV_t+\eta^2d\nu^2\). To prove confinement, set \(b=\eta d\nu^2/\alpha\) and stop using increments as soon as the distance exceeds a radius \(r<\delta\). On a step begun inside the ball, let \(D_{t+1}=V_{t+1}-\mathbb E_tV_{t+1}\), and use zero for that increment after exit. Expanding the update with noise norm bounded by \(Q\) shows \(|D_{t+1}|\le C_1(\eta r+\eta^2)\), with a constant depending on \(Q,L\). On first exit at time \(t\), iterating the drift inequality up to and including that step gives

\[V_t\le a^t V_0+(1-a^t)b+\sum_{j=1}^t a^{t-j}D_j .\]

For each \(t\), the sum uses martingale differences with squared bounds summing to at most \(C_1^2(\eta r+\eta^2)^2/(1-a^2)=O(\eta r^2+\eta^3)\). Now choose \(r^2=K\eta\log(2N/\zeta)\) for a sufficiently large \(K\) and a horizon \(N\). The deterministic terms are \(O(\eta)\) because entry has \(V_0=O(\eta)\). Exiting at time \(t\le N\) requires the sum to exceed \(r^2/2\), which Azuma bounds by \(\exp(-c' r^2/\eta)\) with fixed \(c'>0\). A union bound gives exit probability at most \(\zeta/2\), while the radius stays below \(\delta\) for a sufficiently small step.

Entry and completion. At a block boundary outside the minimum region, take a single large-gradient step or one escape block. Each block has length \(\ell\le T_{\max}=O(\eta^{-1})\) and expected decrease at least \(c''\eta^2\ell\), for a uniform positive constant. Stop adding blocks when an entry is found at a boundary. For each finite number of blocks, summing conditional decreases bounds expected elapsed stopped time by \(2B/(c''\eta^2)\), where \(|f|\le B\). Monotone convergence extends this to the entry time. Markov’s inequality therefore gives entry within \(O(\eta^{-2})\) steps with probability at least \(1/2\) from any starting state. Repeat the estimate at deterministic windows if entry has not occurred; the uniform conditional failure bound gives failure at most \(2^{-q}\) after \(q\) windows. On entry, apply the residence estimate for the remaining time. Taking \(q=O(\log(1/\zeta))\) gives the claimed horizon and radius. This is an equivalent common-noise coupling proof for additive isotropic martingale-difference noise. Ge et al., Escaping from Saddle Points, Definition 5, Theorem 6, Lemmas 7–9, and Appendix A, Theorem 13 and Lemmas 14–18.

Why a matrix completion objective can have no spurious local minima

Ge, Lee, and Ma study PSD observations of \(M=ZZ^\top\) with a symmetric independent-entry mask and row penalty \(R(X)=\sum_i(\|X_i\|-\alpha)_+^4\). The penalty gradient is rowwise radial: at a row above threshold it is \(4(\|X_i\|-\alpha)^3X_i/\|X_i\|\), and it vanishes on all other rows. This follows by differentiating the radial norm. At a stationary point it opposes growth of a large row. For \(E=XX^\top-M\), the unregularized objective \(g(X)=\frac12\|P_\Omega(E)\|_F^2\) has

\[\nabla g(X)=2P_\Omega(E)X,\quad \mathrm d^2g(X)[H,H] =\|P_\Omega(XH^\top+HX^\top)\|_F^2+2\langle P_\Omega(E),HH^\top\rangle.\]

Indeed, the residual at \(X+tH\) is \(E+t(XH^\top+HX^\top)+t^2HH^\top\). The first two Taylor coefficients give the displayed derivatives. With full observations, for rank one these conditions already characterize the minima, as shown in Problem 3. With partial observations, the row penalty and uniform concentration of the mask control which derivative tests can be transferred to the full problem. Ge, Lee, and Ma, Matrix Completion Has No Spurious Local Minimum, §§3–5.

Sampled rank-one recovery

Consider \(F(x)=\frac12\|P_\Omega(xx^\top-zz^\top)\|_F^2+\lambda R(x)\) for \(\|z\|=1\) and \(\|z\|_\infty\le\mu/\sqrt d\). This derivation uses the rank-one structure in §4 of Ge, Lee, and Ma. It gives exact recovery for \(p\ge C\mu^6\log(d+1)/d\) with a sufficiently large universal constant \(C\), in the regime where the condition is feasible. Set \(\alpha=10\mu/\sqrt d\) and \(\lambda\ge p\mu^2/\alpha^2\).

Let \(B_{ij}=\mathbf1\{(i,j)\in\Omega\}\) and \(E_B=B-p\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\). Entries on and above the diagonal of \(B\) are independent Bernoulli\((p)\), with their transposes reflected below it. A Chernoff bound and a union bound give \(\sum_jB_{ij}\le2pd\) for every row with high probability when \(pd\ge C\log(d+1)\). For the spectral estimate, write \(E_B\) as an independent sum of centered matrices \(\xi_{ij}(e_ie_j^\top+e_je_i^\top)\) for \(i<j\) and \(\xi_{ii}e_ie_i^\top\) on the diagonal. Each summand has norm at most 1, and the sum of the expected squares is diagonal with entries \(dp(1-p)\le dp\). Matrix Bernstein gives

\[P(\|E_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\ge t)\le 2d\exp[-t^2/(2dp+2t/3)].\]

Thus \(\|E_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C_0\sqrt{pd\log(d+1)}\) with failure probability at most an inverse polynomial in \(d\). The same event controls the sampled forms uniformly, even when \(x\) is chosen from the observations. For example,

\[\|(P_\Omega(uv^\top)-puv^\top)s\| \le \|u\|_\infty\|E_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|v\odot s\|,\qquad |\langle P_\Omega(uv^\top),ab^\top\rangle-p\langle uv^\top,ab^\top\rangle| \le\|E_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|u\odot a\|\|v\odot b\|.\]

These identities come from writing the centered masked product as \(\operatorname{diag}(u)E_B\operatorname{diag}(v)\). They give a rank-one route to the uniform estimates in Appendix D of the paper.

At a stationary point, a coordinate \(i\) maximizing \(|x_i|=M_x\) gives the row bound. If \(M_x>2\alpha\), multiply the stationarity equation by the sign of \(x_i\). The observed self-product contribution \(2M_x\sum_{j:B_{ij}=1}x_j^2\) is nonnegative. The target term is at most \(4p\mu^2 M_x\) in magnitude by the row count bound and \(|z_i z_j|\le\mu^2/d\). The penalty term is at least \(\lambda M_x^3/2\). Therefore \(M_x^2\le8p\mu^2/\lambda\), and in every case \(\|x\|_\infty\le4\alpha\).

Denote \(\chi=(4\alpha)^2\|E_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}/p\). The sample-size condition lets us make \(\chi\) an arbitrarily small constant. With \(s=\|x\|^2\) and \(a=\langle z,x\rangle\), the vector mask bounds give

\[\left\|a z-sx-\frac{\lambda}{2p}\nabla R(x)\right\|\le2\chi\sqrt s.\]

Indeed, \(\|\operatorname{diag}(z)E_B(z\odot x)\|\le\|z\|_\infty^2\|E_B\|_{\rm op}\|x\|\) and the analogous \(x\) estimate is bounded by \((4\alpha)^2\|E_B\|_{\rm op}\|x\|\). Inner product with \(x\) gives \(s^2\le a^2+2\chi s\le(1+2\chi)s\), because the radial penalty has nonnegative inner product with \(x\). Thus \(s\le1+2\chi\).

Let \(J=\{i:|x_i|>\alpha\}\). The preceding bound implies \(|J|\le s/\alpha^2\) and \(\|z_J\|^2\le s/100\). Use \(v=z_{J^c}\) in the Hessian test. The penalty curvature contributes zero since \(v_i\) vanishes on every coordinate where the Hessian of the penalty is nonzero. The centered mask perturbs the unregularized Hessian quadratic form by at most \((2\|x\|_\infty^2+2\|z\|_\infty^2)\|E_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|v\|^2+2\|E_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|x\odot v\|^2+2\|E_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|x\odot v\|^2\), which is \(O(p\chi)\|v\|^2\). This bound follows term by term from the squared derivative \(\|P_\Omega(xv^\top+vx^\top)\|_F^2\) and the residual term \(2 v^\top P_\Omega(xx^\top-zz^\top)v\). Without the mask the Hessian test equals \(2s\|v\|^2+4\langle x,v\rangle^2-2\langle z,v\rangle^2\). Even if the observed Hessian is only bounded below by \(-\tau I\) with \(\tau\le p/10\), division by \(p\|v\|^2\) yields \(6s\ge2\|v\|^2-\tau/p-O(\chi)\). Here \(\langle z,v\rangle=\|v\|^2\ge1-s/100\). For small enough \(\chi\), this forces \(s\ge1/4\).

On \(J\), \(|a z_i|\le\sqrt s\,\mu/\sqrt d\) while \(s|x_i|\ge10s\,\mu/\sqrt d\). Since \(s\ge1/4\), the latter dominates. Hence on those coordinates \(a z_i-sx_i\) has sign opposite to the regularizer gradient, whose sign agrees with \(x_i\). Removing the penalty from the vector bound cannot increase its left side. On \(J^c\) the penalty derivative is already zero. We obtain \(\|a z-sx\|\le2\chi\sqrt s\). Its inner products with \(x\) and \(z\) imply \(|a^2-s^2|\le2\chi s\) and \(|a(1-s)|\le2\chi\sqrt s\). For sufficiently small \(\chi\), \(s\ge1/4\) now also bounds \(|a|\) below by a positive constant. It follows that \(s=1+O(\chi)\) and \(|a|=1+O(\chi)\). After changing the sign of \(z\) if needed, \(\|x-z\|^2=s+1-2|a|=O(\chi)\).

To finish with exactness, set \(e=x-z\), \(T=ze^\top+ez^\top\) and \(Q=ee^\top\). For \(e\) small, the quadratic residual \(P_\Omega(Q)\) is dominated by the linear residual \(P_\Omega(T)\). First, a second use of the multiplicative Chernoff bound gives \(\sum_i B_{ij} z_i^2\ge p/2\) for every \(j\), with failure probability at most \(d\exp(-c pd/\mu^2)\). This is the weighted Bernoulli bound obtained by rescaling the weights by \(\mu^2/d\) so the summands lie in \([0,1]\) and using their total mean. Expand the squared norm of \(P_\Omega(T)\). Its two squared terms sum to at least \(p\|e\|^2\) by that row event. The cross term is \(2p(z^\top e)^2+2(z\odot e)^\top E_B(z\odot e)\), lower bounded by \(-2\|z\|_\infty^2\|E_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|e\|^2\). Hence \(\|P_\Omega(T)\|_F^2\ge(p/2)\|e\|^2\). Also

\[\|P_\Omega(Q)\|_F^2 =p\|e\|^4+(e\odot e)^\top E_B(e\odot e) \le[p\|e\|^2+\|E_B\|_{\rm op}\|e\|_\infty^2]\|e\|^2.\]

Because \(\|e\|_\infty\le5\alpha\) and \(\|e\|^2=O(\chi)\), the ratio of the two squared residual norms is \(O(\chi)\) when \(e\ne0\). Choose \(\chi\) small enough that \(\|P_\Omega(Q)\|_F\le\|P_\Omega(T)\|_F/4\). The directional derivative in the direction \(e\) is

\[\langle P_\Omega(T+Q),P_\Omega(T+2Q)\rangle+\lambda\langle\nabla R(x),e\rangle .\]

The first term is at least \((1-3/4)\|P_\Omega(T)\|_F^2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz. The second is nonnegative since any penalized \(x_i\) has \(x_i-z_i\) of the same sign. Unless \(e=0\) the directional derivative is strictly positive. This contradicts stationarity and proves exact recovery. All local minima meet the two derivative conditions, and \(\pm z\) attain zero objective. Ge, Lee, and Ma, Theorem 4.2, Lemmas 4.3–4.10, and Appendices C–E.

Sampled positive-semidefinite completion at higher rank

We give the local-minimum proof for full-column-rank \(Z\in\mathbb R^{d\times r}\) in Ge, Lee, and Ma, §5. Use their normalization \(\|Z\|_F^2=r\), incoherence bound \(\max_i\|Z_i\|\le\mu\sqrt{r/d}\), and condition number \(\kappa=\sigma_{\max}(Z)/\sigma_{\min}(Z)\). Set \(M=ZZ^\top\), \(R(X)=\sum_i(\|X_i\|-\alpha)_+^4\), and \(f(X)=\tfrac12\|\mathcal P_\Omega(XX^\top-M)\|_F^2+\lambda R(X)\). Under the symmetric Bernoulli sampling model, Theorem 5.3 uses \(p\ge C d^{-1}\log^2 d\max(\mu^6\kappa^{16}r^4,\mu^4\kappa^4r^6)\), \(\alpha=32\mu\kappa r/\sqrt d\), and \(\lambda\ge\mu^2rp/\alpha^2\). The proof below separates the sampling estimates from the algebraic implications at a local minimum. Ge, Lee, and Ma, Propositions 5.1–5.2, Theorem 5.3, and Appendices C–D.

Stationarity controls row norms. The regularizer gradient is \(\Gamma X\) for a diagonal \(\Gamma\) with \(\Gamma_{ii}=4(\|X_i\|-\alpha)_+^3/\|X_i\|\) at nonzero rows and zero otherwise. If \(X_i\) has maximum norm \(s\), the row of \(\mathcal P_\Omega(M)X\) has norm at most \(2p\mu^2rs\) on the event that row \(i\) has at most \(2pd\) samples. Indeed, \(|M_{ij}|\le\mu^2r/d\) and each \(\|X_j\|\le s\). The inner product of \((\mathcal P_\Omega(XX^\top)X)_i\) with \(X_i\) is \(\sum_{j\in\Omega_i}\langle X_j,X_i\rangle^2\ge0\). Project the stationarity equation onto the unit vector along \(X_i\). This gives \(4\lambda(s-\alpha)_+^3\le4p\mu^2rs\). If \(s\ge2\alpha\), then \((s-\alpha)^3\ge s^3/8\), so \(s^2\le8p\mu^2r/\lambda\). The assumed \(\lambda\) therefore bounds \(s\) by \(4\alpha\).

Replace sampled products by full products. The uniform cubic estimate in Theorem D.2 controls \(\mathcal P_\Omega(WW^\top)W-pWW^\top W\) for row-incoherent \(W\). For example, with fixed \(\|W\|_F=1\), the \(i\)th row is a sum of centered terms \((\mathbf1_{\{ij\in\Omega\}}-p)\langle W_i,W_j\rangle W_j\) of norm at most \(\nu^3/d^{3/2}\) when \(\|W_i\|\le\nu/\sqrt d\). Its expected squared norm is at most \(p\nu^6/d^2\). The proof truncates using the uniform row-degree bound, concentrates the sum of squared row deviations, and unions over a Frobenius net with \(\exp(O(dr\log d))\) points; the cubic product is Lipschitz on the normalized bounded region. To pass from independent directed entries to symmetric entries the paper averages a directed mask and its transpose. This has the law of the average of independent symmetric masks off the diagonal. The triangle inequality for three independent masks bounds the tail of one mask in terms of the tail of the sum of two (Claim D.3). The mixed-product estimates used in the stationarity equations are those of Lemma 5.5.

Here is how those estimates constrain \(X\). For \(A=\mathcal P_\Omega(XX^\top)X\), the row calculation above gives \(\langle A,\Gamma X\rangle\ge0\). Thus \(\|A+(\lambda/2)\Gamma X\|_F\ge\|A\|_F\). Substituting stationarity and comparing sampled and full products bounds \(\|XX^\top X\|_F\) by \(\|ZZ^\top X\|_F\) plus the concentration errors. Write the singular values of \(X\) as \(s_j\). The left squared norm is \(\sum_j s_j^6\), whereas the right squared norm before the error is at most \(\sigma_{\max}(Z)^4\sum_j s_j^2\). Apply \(\sum_j s_j^6\ge(\sum_j s_j^2)^3/r^2\) and \(\max_j s_j^6\le\sum_j s_j^6\). This gives the bounds in Lemma 5.5, \(\|X\|_F^2\le 2r\sigma_{\max}(Z)^2\) and \(\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2r^{1/6}\sigma_{\max}(Z)\), together with a small residual in the full first-order equation.

Second-order test. Put \(J=\{i:\|X_i\|\le\alpha\}\) and let \(Z_J\) agree with \(Z\) on \(J\) and vanish elsewhere. Since \(|J^c|\alpha^2\le\|X\|_F^2\), \(\|Z-Z_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sqrt{|J^c|}\mu\sqrt{r/d}\). The chosen threshold and the Frobenius bound ensure \(\sigma_{\min}(Z_J)\ge \sigma_{\min}(Z)/2\). Let \(v\) be a unit right singular vector of \(X\) for its smallest singular value. There is a unit \(z\) in the column space of \(Z_J\) with \(\|X^\top z\|\le\sigma_{\min}(X)\): compress \(XX^\top\) to an \(r\)-dimensional subspace and use the min–max characterization of its \(r\)th largest eigenvalue. The regularizer contributes zero curvature on the direction \(H=zv^\top\) because its active rows lie outside \(J\) and the quartic has zero second derivative at the threshold. The sampled Hessian test is

\[\|\mathcal P_\Omega(z w^\top+w z^\top)\|_F^2 \ge 2\langle\mathcal P_\Omega(M-XX^\top),zz^\top\rangle,\qquad w=Xv.\]

The bilinear estimate D.1 transfers the test to the full inner product with additive error \(pe\) at the paper’s sampling rate. Its proof starts with Bernstein for a fixed pair: with unit Frobenius norms and entry bounds \(a,b\), the summands are bounded by \(2ab\) and their aggregate variance is at most \(2pab\), merging symmetric pairs. A union bound over a rank-\(O(r)\) Frobenius net of size \(\exp(O(dr\log d))\) and a Frobenius Lipschitz extension give \(O(ab\,dr\log d+\sqrt{pab\,dr\log d})\) uniformly. Apply this to the two products in the Hessian expression. After dividing the Hessian inequality by \(2p\), expand the rank-two square:

\[\|w\|^2+\langle z,w\rangle^2+\|X^\top z\|^2 \ge \|Z^\top z\|^2-e/2.\]

The left is at most \(3\sigma_{\min}(X)^2\), while the right is at least \(\sigma_{\min}(Z_J)^2-e/2\). Making the sampling error sufficiently small proves that \(\sigma_{\min}(X)\) is bounded below by a constant times \(\sigma_{\min}(Z)\), as in Lemma 5.6.

Remove the row penalty and recover the Gram matrix. Write \(B=ZZ^\top X-XX^\top X\). The residual from the first-order condition controls \(B-(\lambda/2p)\Gamma X\). On a row with positive \(\Gamma_{ii}\), the negative term in \(\langle B_i,X_i\rangle\) has magnitude at least \(\sigma_{\min}(X)^2\|X_i\|^2\). The positive term is at most \(\|X_i\|\|Z_i\|\|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). With the row threshold and the bounds of Lemmas 5.5–5.6 the negative term dominates, giving \(\langle B,\Gamma X\rangle\le0\) (Lemma 5.7). Expanding a squared norm now gives \(\|B\|_F^2\le\|B-(\lambda/2p)\Gamma X\|_F^2\).

Decompose \(Z=U+V\) with \(U=P_{\mathrm{col}(X)}Z\) and \(V=(I-P_{\mathrm{col}(X)})Z\). Since \(V^\top X=0\),

\[\|B\|_F^2=\|(UU^\top-XX^\top)X\|_F^2+\|VU^\top X\|_F^2.\]

These terms are orthogonal in their left column spaces. If \(\|B\|_F\le\eta\), invert \(X\) on its column space to obtain \(\|UU^\top-XX^\top\|_F\le\eta/\sigma_{\min}(X)\). Also \(\sigma_{\min}(UU^\top X)\ge\sigma_{\min}(X)^3-\eta\), so \(\sigma_{\min}(U^\top X)\ge(\sigma_{\min}(X)^3-\eta)/\|U\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). For \(\eta<\sigma_{\min}(X)^3/2\), this bounds \(\|V\|_F\le2\eta\|U\|_{\mathrm{op}}/\sigma_{\min}(X)^3\). Finally expand \(ZZ^\top=UU^\top+UV^\top+VU^\top+VV^\top\) and use \(\|UV^\top\|_F\le\|U\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|V\|_F\), \(\|VV^\top\|_F\le\|V\|_F^2\). This is the Gram-error control of Lemma 5.8.

Local exactness. Let \(\rho=\|XX^\top-M\|_F\) be sufficiently small. Write \(M=\operatorname{diag}(D,0)\) in an orthonormal basis and \(X=[A^\top,W^\top]^\top\) in that basis. Then \(\|AA^\top-D\|_F\le\rho\) and \(\|WA^\top\|_F\le\rho\). The first shows \(\sigma_{\min}(A)\ge(\sigma_{\min}(Z)^2-\rho)^{1/2}\); the second gives \(\|W\|_F\le\rho/\sigma_{\min}(A)\). Choose an exact factor \(Y=[(AS)^\top,0]^\top\) with \(S=(A^\top D^{-1}A)^{-1/2}\), so \(YY^\top=M\). The eigenvalues of \(A^\top D^{-1}A\) differ from one by at most \(\rho/\sigma_{\min}(Z)^2\), since they are those of \(D^{-1/2}AA^\top D^{-1/2}\). On this interval the derivative of \(t^{-1/2}\) is bounded, so \(\|S-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O(\rho/\sigma_{\min}(Z)^2)\). Consequently \(\|X-Y\|_F=O(\rho\sqrt r/\sigma_{\min}(Z)^2)\), as in Claim C.4.

Put \(D_1=Y(X-Y)^\top+(X-Y)Y^\top\) and \(D_2=(X-Y)(X-Y)^\top\). Then the residual is \(D_1+D_2\), and the directional derivative of \(XX^\top\) along \(X-Y\) is \(D_1+2D_2\). Appendix C uses two sampling estimates: \(\|\mathcal P_\Omega D_1\|_F^2\ge(5p/6)\|D_1\|_F^2\) on the tangent space (Lemma C.6), and \(\|\mathcal P_\Omega D_2\|_F^2\le(p/25)\rho^2\) for the controlled near-solution region (Lemma C.5, citing Sun and Luo’s Proposition 4.3). Since \(\|D_2\|_F\le\|X-Y\|_F^2\le\rho/10\) in that region, \(\|D_1\|_F\ge9\rho/10\). Write \(a=\|\mathcal P_\Omega D_1\|_F\) and \(b=\|\mathcal P_\Omega D_2\|_F\). The loss derivative is at least \(a^2-3ab+2b^2=(a-b)(a-2b)\). Since \(a\ge\sqrt{27/40}\sqrt p\,\rho\) and \(b\le\sqrt p\,\rho/5\), it is greater than \(p\rho^2/4\) when \(\rho>0\). The regularizer contributes a nonnegative derivative: \(Y=ZQ\) for an orthogonal \(Q\) so its rows have the same norms as those of \(Z\), below \(\alpha\). For \(\Gamma_{ii}>0\), \(\langle X_i,X_i-Y_i\rangle\ge\|X_i\|(\|X_i\|-\|Y_i\|)>0\). Stationarity therefore forces \(\rho=0\). The exact factors have zero regularizer as well, so the objective is zero. Ge, Lee, and Ma, Lemma C.1 · Sun and Luo, §4.1 and Proposition 4.3

Why the two local sampling estimates hold

Let \(T\) be the symmetric tangent space \(\{YH^\top+HY^\top\}\), where \(Y\) is an exact factor of \(M\). With \(P\) the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{col}(Y)\), the Frobenius orthogonal projection onto \(T\) sends a symmetric matrix \(B\) to \(PB+BP-PBP\). This follows by writing \(B\) in the orthogonal decomposition given by \(P\): it preserves all blocks except the lower-right block. In the orthonormal basis of symmetric coordinate matrices \(E_a\) (off-diagonal entries use \((e_ie_j^\top+e_je_i^\top)/\sqrt2\)), put \(u_a=P_T E_a\). From \(\|Pe_i\|\le\|Z_i\|/\sigma_{\min}(Z)\) we have \(\max_a\|u_a\|_F^2\le C\mu^2r/(d\,\sigma_{\min}(Z)^2)=:L_T\). Moreover, \(\sum_a u_a\otimes u_a=I_T\), because expanding any tangent vector in the coordinate basis and projecting back leaves it unchanged.

The random quadratic form measuring tangent energy has operator \(\sum_a \xi_a u_a\otimes u_a\), where the independent \(\xi_a\) are the Bernoulli observations. After centering, each summand has norm at most \(L_T\), and the sum of its operator variances is bounded above by \(pL_T I_T\) since \((u_a\otimes u_a)^2=\|u_a\|^2(u_a\otimes u_a)\). Matrix Bernstein at deviation \(p/6\) gives probability at most \(2\dim(T)\exp(-c p/L_T)\) of deviating that far from \(p I_T\). This proves the tangent lower bound used above on a single event for all tangent directions and all exact factors, since they have the same column space.

For the quadratic residual, write \(H=X-Y\) and let \(S\) denote the matrix of centered Bernoulli indicators. Define a matrix \(F\) whose \(i\)th row is \(H_i\otimes H_i\). Then \(\big((HH^\top)_{ij}\big)^2=(FF^\top)_{ij}\). Consequently

\[\|\mathcal P_\Omega(HH^\top)\|_F^2 =p\|HH^\top\|_F^2+\operatorname{tr}(F^\top S F) \le p\|H\|_F^4+\|S\|_{\mathrm{op}}\max_i\|H_i\|^2\|H\|_F^2.\]

The inequality uses \(\|F\|_F^2=\sum_i\|H_i\|^4\). For the symmetric mask write \(S\) as a sum of independently centered diagonal and off-diagonal coordinate matrices. Each summand has norm at most one and the operator variance is diagonal with entries at most \(pd\). Hence matrix Bernstein gives \(\|S\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C(\sqrt{pd\log d}+\log d)\) with probability at least \(1-d^{-10}\) after choosing the absolute constant. Substituting the row bound \(\max_i\|H_i\|\le4\alpha+\mu\sqrt{r/d}\) and \(\|H\|_F=O(\rho\sqrt r/\sigma_{\min}(Z)^2)\) makes the right side at most \(p\rho^2/25\) whenever the resulting coefficients satisfy

\[\left(\frac{\|H\|_F^2}{\rho}\right)^2+ \frac{\|S\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{p}\max_i\|H_i\|^2 \left(\frac{\|H\|_F}{\rho}\right)^2\le\frac1{25}.\]

This shows explicitly how closeness, uniform row control, and sampling concentration enter the quadratic estimate. The proof of the full sampling range in Lemma C.5 uses Sun and Luo’s Proposition 4.3, which gives that estimate on the controlled region without imposing the sufficient condition above.

Challenges

Problem 1

Suppose every observed value of a nonzero rank-one matrix is nonzero. Give necessary and sufficient graph conditions for unique rank-one completion among real matrices, including vertices with no observed edges.

Show solution

View rows and columns as vertices of a bipartite graph with observed entries as edges. If the graph is connected, fix one nonzero factor coordinate as a gauge and propagate each adjacent factor by dividing the edge value by the known coordinate. Existence of the given completion ensures consistency around cycles. All factors are then determined up to one global rescaling and every product is fixed. If an isolated row or column is present, its factor can vary while preserving the observations and changing at least one unobserved product. If two components contain edges, independently rescale the row factors in one by \(c\) and the column factors there by \(1/c\). Cross-component entries change. In the no-observation case a real rank-one completion is plainly nonunique.

Problem 2

A \(4\times4\) matrix has its first two rows equal to \((9,0,1,0)\) and the other two equal to \((2,2,2,2)\). Compute its smallest joint incoherence constant without an SVD.

Show solution

The column projector has constant diagonal \(1/2\). For the row projector, form \(B\) from the distinct rows and use \(B^\top(BB^\top)^{-1}B\). The inner inverse is \(\frac1{912}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}16&-20\\-20&82\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). The four column quadratic forms give the diagonal \((113,41,33,41)/114\). The rank is 2, so the joint constant is \((4/2)\max\{1/2,113/114\}=113/57\).

Problem 3. A second-order proof of exact rank-one recovery

Let \(z\) be a unit vector and \(g(x)=\tfrac14\|xx^\top-zz^\top\|_F^2\). Prove from first- and second-order conditions that every local minimizer equals \(z\) or \(-z\). Use only the gradient inner products with \(x,z\) and the Hessian quadratic form in direction \(z\). These are the tests later compared to their sampled counterparts. Ge, Lee, and Ma, §3, Lemma 3.2 and Claims 1f–2f.

Show solution

Put \(s=\|x\|^2\) and \(a=\langle z,x\rangle\). Expanding the Frobenius norm gives \(g=(s^2-2a^2+1)/4\), \(\nabla g=sx-az\), and \(\nabla^2 g=sI+2xx^\top-zz^\top\). Its quadratic form at \(z\) is \(s+2a^2-1\). Positivity and \(a^2\le s\) imply \(s\ge1/3\). Taking the gradient inner product with \(x\) yields \(s^2=a^2\), hence \(a\ne0\). Taking it with \(z\) yields \(a(s-1)=0\), so \(s=1\) and \(a^2=1\). Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz forces \(x=\pm z\). Both are global minima with residual zero.

Problem 4. A certificate for unique nuclear norm completion

Write \(M=U\Sigma V^\top\) and let \(T=\{UA^\top+BV^\top\}\). Assume \(P_\Omega\) is injective on \(T\), and some \(Y=P_\Omega Y\) satisfies \(P_TY=UV^\top\) and \(\|P_{T^\perp}Y\|_{\rm op}<1\). Prove \(M\) is the unique minimizer of nuclear norm subject to the observations. Candès and Recht, Exact Matrix Completion via Convex Optimization, §3, dual uniqueness argument.

Show solution

For feasible \(M+H\), \(P_\Omega H=0\), so \(\langle Y,H\rangle=0\). Let \(W\) be the product of the singular-vector matrices from a thin SVD of \(P_{T^\perp}H\) (or zero if that projection is zero). Its row and column spaces lie in the orthogonal complements of \(V\) and \(U\). Thus \(UV^\top+W\) has spectral norm at most one by orthogonality, and \(\langle W,H\rangle=\|P_{T^\perp}H\|_\ast\). Nuclear/spectral norm duality gives

\[\|M+H\|_\ast-\|M\|_\ast \ge \langle UV^\top+W,H\rangle \ge (1-\|P_{T^\perp}Y\|_{\rm op})\|P_{T^\perp}H\|_\ast.\]

The second inequality uses \(P_TY=UV^\top\) and subtracts the zero inner product with \(Y\). If \(P_{T^\perp}H\ne0\) the objective strictly increases. Otherwise \(H\in T\) and injectivity with \(P_\Omega H=0\) gives \(H=0\).
