ML 11: SimCLR, t-SNE, and Rotary Position Embeddings

Derive the contrastive and t-SNE gradients, connect augmentation pairs to graph energy, and prove why RoPE depends explicitly on relative position.

A two-dimensional query and key rotate at a shared frequency. Their relative angle changes by the difference of the position rotations.

SimCLR and positive-pair graphs

Lecture 11, slides 3–7 and 10–15. The full-vocabulary model below follows Tan et al., Sections 3–4.

Recall why spectral clustering uses the eigenvectors of smallest eigenvalue. For a symmetric nonnegative weight matrix \(A\) and \(L=\operatorname{diag}(A\mathbf 1)-A\), write \(L=V\Lambda V^\top\), with \(\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_n\). For \(U^\top U=I_k\), let \(r_\ell\) be the squared norm of row \(\ell\) of \(V^\top U\). Then \(0\le r_\ell\le1\) because \(V^\top UU^\top V\) is an orthogonal projection, \(\sum_\ell r_\ell=k\) by its trace, and \(\operatorname{tr}(U^\top LU)=\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell r_\ell\). Moving mass from any larger occupied \(\lambda_\ell\) to a smaller unsaturated one cannot increase this sum. Its minimum is \(\sum_{\ell=1}^k\lambda_\ell\), attained by taking the corresponding eigenvectors. The row of \(U\) for object \(i\) is a \(k\)-dimensional embedding. Apply \(k\)-means to those rows and transfer the resulting labels to the original objects.

Apply two independent random augmentations to each image. Each such pair consists of positive views, while the other images in the batch supply negative views. An encoder provides \(h_i\), and a projection head provides unit embeddings \(z_i\). For anchor \(i\) and its positive partner \(j\), define

\[\ell_{i,j}=-\log\frac{\exp(z_i^\top z_j/\tau)}{\sum_{k\ne i}\exp(z_i^\top z_k/\tau)}.\]

Write \(s_{ik}=z_i^\top z_k\) and \(p_{ik}\) for the temperature softmax. Differentiating the positive linear term and the log normalizer gives \(\partial\ell_{i,j}/\partial s_{ik}=(p_{ik}-\mathbf1\{k=j\})/\tau\). High-similarity negative examples receive a larger repulsive derivative. The actual encoder gradient couples many pairs through shared parameters. SimCLR uses both directions of each positive pair and commonly uses the encoder’s pre-projection representation downstream.

In a finite vocabulary of possible augmented views, put a symmetric edge weight on each positive pair proportional to its frequency. With embedding matrix \(Z\) and Laplacian \(L\), expanding squared edge differences gives

\[\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}\|z_i-z_j\|^2=\operatorname{tr}(Z^\top LZ).\]

With unit embeddings, minimizing this term is equivalent to maximizing the weighted dot products over positive edges. The InfoNCE normalizer adds a second term. For example, with ordered positive-pair probabilities \(\pi_{ij}\) and marginals \(\pi_i=\sum_j\pi_{ij}\), the full-vocabulary loss is \(-\tau^{-1}\sum_{ij}\pi_{ij}s_{ij}+\sum_i\pi_i\log\sum_{k\ne i}e^{s_{ik}/\tau}\). The graph energy accounts for the attraction term. Spectral equivalence for a complete contrastive objective depends on how subgraphs, kernel probabilities, and normalization are modeled. Image–text contrastive learning instead uses a bipartite positive graph.

From an RKHS Gram matrix to a subgraph distribution

Let \(K_{ij}=k(z_i,z_j)=\langle\varphi(z_i),\varphi(z_j)\rangle_{\mathcal H}\) for a positive semidefinite kernel \(k\). In its RKHS one can take \(\varphi(z)=k(z,\cdot)\), since reproducing function evaluation gives \(\langle k(z,\cdot),k(z',\cdot)\rangle=k(z,z')\). For any coefficient vector \(c\), \(c^\top Kc=\|\sum_i c_i\varphi(z_i)\|_{\mathcal H}^2\ge0\). The finite Gram matrix remains computable even when \(\mathcal H\) is infinite-dimensional. The graph probability also requires nonnegative allowed edge weights. Positive semidefiniteness alone does not ensure nonnegative entries.

For the Gaussian weights used here, nonnegativity is immediate. Positive semidefiniteness follows from

\[e^{-\|z-z'\|^2/(2\tau)} =e^{-\|z\|^2/(2\tau)}e^{-\|z'\|^2/(2\tau)} \sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle z,z'\rangle^r}{r!\tau^r}.\]

Each dot-product power is the inner product of \(r\)-fold tensor powers, and scaling a feature vector by the leading exponential gives the outside factors. Directly summing these weighted tensor features proves the Gram property. SimCLR uses unit vectors, so \(\exp(-\|z-z'\|^2/(2\tau))=\exp(-1/\tau)\exp(z^\top z'/\tau)\). The constant cancels in each ratio.

Deriving InfoNCE from the graph cross-entropy

For each directed edge from \(i\) to \(j\), let \(w_{ij}\) be its nonnegative score and exclude self-edges. Impose the constraint that every row selects one outgoing edge. A graph is then determined by its choices \(j(i)\), and its unnormalized probability is \(\prod_i w_{i,j(i)}\). Since the constraint on one row is independent of the other rows, summing this product over all graphs gives \(\prod_i\sum_{k\ne i}w_{ik}\). The resulting probability of the graph factors into the product of its row probabilities.

Take the positive-pair weights to be \(\pi_{ij}\) and the embedding weights to be \(w_{ij}=\exp(z_i^\top z_j/\tau)\). Write \(\pi(j\mid i)=\pi_{ij}/\sum_{k\ne i}\pi_{ik}\) for every row with positive total weight. The negative log probability under the embedding distribution, averaged over the positive-pair graph, is

\[-\sum_i\sum_{j\ne i}\pi(j\mid i) \log\frac{\exp(z_i^\top z_j/\tau)}{\sum_{k\ne i}\exp(z_i^\top z_k/\tau)}.\]

This is the sum of per-anchor InfoNCE objectives, averaged over each anchor’s positives. Weighting the rows by their anchor frequencies gives the ordered-pair objective above. The derivation requires the one-outgoing-edge constraint and these exponential weights. For a graph model that couples row choices, the partition function need not factor.

Recovering the Laplacian term and the role of the partition function

For either graph, write the full law as

\[P_B(W)=\frac{\Omega(W)\prod_{i,j\ne i}B_{ij}^{W_{ij}}}{\mathcal Z_B}, \qquad \mathcal Z_B=\prod_i\sum_{j\ne i}B_{ij}.\]

Here \(B=\pi\) produces the augmentation graph, \(B=K\) produces the embedding graph, and \(\Omega\) enforces one outgoing edge per row. Only used edges enter the product. Put \(P_{ij}=\mathbb E_{\pi} W_{ij}=\pi(j\mid i)\), and use Gaussian \(K_{ij}\). Taking the negative logarithm and then expectation under \(P_\pi\) gives

\[H(P_\pi,P_K)=\frac{1}{2\tau}\sum_{i,j\ne i}P_{ij}\|z_i-z_j\|^2 +\sum_i\log\sum_{k\ne i}e^{-\|z_i-z_k\|^2/(2\tau)}.\]

The distance is symmetric in the edge endpoints, hence we may replace \(P\) in the first sum by \(A=(P+P^\top)/2\). Expanding the square gives that sum as \(2\operatorname{tr}(Z^\top L_A Z)\), so the first term is \(\operatorname{tr}(Z^\top L_A Z)/\tau\). For symmetric \(P\), \(A=P\) exactly. This is Tan et al.’s regularized spectral formulation in the finite full-vocabulary setting. The log partition function still depends on \(Z\), and ordinary Laplacian eigendecomposition minimizes only a relaxed trace objective. At collapse the trace term is zero, but every row predicts a uniform neighbor and its loss is \(\log(n-1)\).

CLIP uses a bipartite candidate graph

Lecture 11, slide 16. Tan et al., Section 5.

Let \(M_{ij}\) count the observed pairings of image \(i\) and text \(j\). Image anchors draw texts with probabilities \(M_{ij}/\sum_j M_{ij}\). Text anchors use \(M_{ij}/\sum_i M_{ij}\). Use kernel weights only between the two modalities. On each image row the kernel partition sums over all texts, and on each text row it sums over all images. The graph cross-entropy therefore splits into the two directional contrastive losses. On a batch of \(N\) paired and indexed image/text views, averaging their \(2N\) row losses gives CLIP’s symmetric contrastive objective. A vocabulary of deduplicated objects can have unequal degrees. Uniform objects and uniform edges induce different anchor frequencies, so the finite theory needs the sampling convention stated.

t-SNE preserves a distribution over neighbors

Lecture 11, slides 18–25. The original objective and gradient are in van der Maaten and Hinton, Sections 2–3.

For high-dimensional inputs use

\[p_{j\mid i}= \frac{e^{-\|x_i-x_j\|^2/(2\sigma_i^2)}}{\sum_{k\ne i}e^{-\|x_i-x_k\|^2/(2\sigma_i^2)}},\qquad p_{ij}=\frac{p_{j\mid i}+p_{i\mid j}}{2n}.\]

Tune \(\sigma_i\) so that the row perplexity, \(2\) raised to its base-2 entropy, matches the desired effective neighborhood size. For low-dimensional coordinates let \(w_{ij}=(1+\|y_i-y_j\|^2)^{-1}\) and \(q_{ij}=w_{ij}/Z\), \(Z=\sum_{a\ne b}w_{ab}\). Self-pairs have probability zero. The heavy tail leaves room for moderate and large distances and reduces crowding in the embedding.

To differentiate \(D_{\rm KL}(P\|Q)\), omit the constant \(\sum p_{ij}\log p_{ij}\) and write the remainder as \(\log Z-\sum p_{ij}\log w_{ij}\). For \(j\ne i\), \(\nabla_{y_i}\log w_{ij}=-2w_{ij}(y_i-y_j)\). Each unordered pair appears twice. The two terms therefore combine to

\[\nabla_{y_i}D_{\rm KL}(P\|Q)=4\sum_{j\ne i}(p_{ij}-q_{ij})w_{ij}(y_i-y_j).\]

A pair with \(p_{ij}>q_{ij}\) attracts under gradient descent. Distances between clusters and relative cluster areas can vary considerably with the initialization and perplexity.

For the original SNE, the low-dimensional conditional distribution uses Gaussian weights \(q_{j\mid i}\propto e^{-\|y_i-y_j\|^2}\), and the loss is \(\sum_i D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\cdot\mid i}\|q_{\cdot\mid i})\). In symmetric t-SNE each conditional row sums to one, so summing the displayed \(p_{ij}\) over ordered distinct pairs gives \((n+n)/(2n)=1\). This is why the symmetric KL and its derivative use a single global normalizer.

To see the geometric source of crowding, note that a radius-\(r\) ball in \(D\) dimensions has volume proportional to \(r^D\) by the Jacobian of scaling. Doubling radius multiplies local capacity by \(2^{10}\) in ten dimensions and by \(4\) in two. Uniform local density is an illustration, and the exponent describes how rapidly available space expands. At distance \(r\), t-SNE’s unnormalized weight is asymptotic to \(r^{-2}\), while the Gaussian weight decays as \(e^{-r^2}\). Thus a distant pair can move much farther for a given small relative weight under the heavy-tailed kernel. At the origin \((1+r^2)^{-1}=1-r^2+O(r^4)\) has finite weight and zero slope. The distribution works for a chosen embedding dimension above two as well.

RoPE is a representation of position offsets

Lecture 11, slides 26–33. Su et al., Sections 3.2–3.4.

Represent a real block \((a,b)\) by \(a+ib\). Then

\[\overline{(a+ib)}(c+id)=(ac+bd)+i(ad-bc),\]

so the ordinary dot product is its real part. Multiplying by \(e^{im\theta}=\cos(m\theta)+i\sin(m\theta)\) acts on real coordinates via the rotation matrix below. For complex query and key blocks \(q,k\), their rotated Euclidean dot product is \(\operatorname{Re}(\overline q\,k\,e^{i(n-m)\theta})\) because conjugation reverses the query rotation. This derives the relative-offset form without losing the distinction between the complex product and the real score.

In each two-dimensional coordinate block, apply \(R(m\theta_j)\) to query at position \(m\) and \(R(n\theta_j)\) to key at position \(n\). Here \(R(\alpha)\) is the rotation with rows \((\cos\alpha,-\sin\alpha)\) and \((\sin\alpha,\cos\alpha)\). Since \(R(\alpha)^\top=R(-\alpha)\) and \(R(a)R(b)=R(a+b)\),

\[(R(m\theta_j)q)^\top R(n\theta_j)k=q^\top R((n-m)\theta_j)k.\]

Thus the positional action on the dot product is a function of the relative position. The content vectors can still depend on the surrounding context. Multiple blocks use different frequencies, for example \(\theta_j=10000^{-2j/d}\), so they encode different offset scales.

In dimension \(d\) even, let \(\mathcal R_m=\operatorname{diag}(R(m\theta_1),\dots,R(m\theta_{d/2}))\) and \(\theta_j=10000^{-2(j-1)/d}\). Matrices in different blocks act independently, so \(\mathcal R_m^\top\mathcal R_n=\mathcal R_{n-m}\). The full score is \(x_m^\top W_q^\top\mathcal R_{n-m}W_kx_n\) and each rotation preserves norm. With \(d=100\), the first three frequencies are \(1\), approximately \(0.8318\), and approximately \(0.6918\). Large frequencies change quickly with position, and small frequencies have longer periods. An individual block wraps modulo \(2\pi\). Multiple frequencies can still distinguish offsets after a single block wraps, and even a single irrational frequency need not repeat exactly on integer offsets.

Challenge

Prove row perplexity is nondecreasing as Gaussian bandwidth grows. Under what conditions is it strictly increasing?

Show solution

Write \(\beta=1/(2\sigma_i^2)\), \(a_j=\|x_i-x_j\|^2\), and \(p_j=e^{-\beta a_j}/Z\). In natural units \(H=\log Z+\beta\mathbb E_pa\). Differentiate: \((\log Z)'=-\mathbb E_pa\) and \((\mathbb E_pa)'=-\operatorname{Var}_p(a)\). Therefore \(dH/d\beta=-\beta\operatorname{Var}_p(a)\). Increasing \(\sigma_i\) decreases \(\beta\), so \(e^H\) is nondecreasing. For finite positive bandwidth and at least two unequal distances the variance is positive, making the increase strict. If all distances are equal, the distribution is always uniform.

Second challenge. Suppose a single RoPE block uses angle \(\theta\) with \(\theta/2\pi\) irrational. Can two integer offsets give exactly the same rotation? Can their rotations be arbitrarily close?

Show solution to the second challenge

Equality would require \((m-n)\theta\in2\pi\mathbb Z\), which is impossible for different integer offsets. For closeness, divide the circle into \(N\) intervals and place the \(N+1\) angles \(0,\theta,\ldots,N\theta\) into them modulo \(2\pi\). Two fall into the same interval, so the nonzero difference has angular distance at most \(2\pi/N\) from zero. Exact injectivity therefore gives no uniform numerical separation over unbounded positions.

Research challenge — the large-negative limit of contrastive learning

In Wang and Isola’s Theorem 1, embeddings lie on the unit sphere. In this exercise the encoder and temperature \(\tau>0\) are fixed. Draw \((X,Y)\) from a positive-pair distribution with common marginal \(\mu\), and draw \(X_1^-,\ldots,X_M^-\) independently from \(\mu\), independent of the pair. Let \(s(x,y)=f(x)^\top f(y)/\tau\). Prove that

\[\lim_{M\to\infty}\left[ \mathbb E\left(-s(X,Y)+\log\left(e^{s(X,Y)}+ \sum_{j=1}^M e^{s(X,X_j^-)}\right)\right)-\log M \right] =-\mathbb E s(X,Y)+\mathbb E_X\log\mathbb E_{Z\sim\mu} e^{s(X,Z)}.\]

Give a direct \(O(M^{-1/2})\) bound at fixed \(\tau\) and rewrite the two limiting terms using squared distances. Why does the second term contain an expectation of a logarithm?

Show research challenge solution

Condition on \(X,Y\) and write \(Y_j=e^{s(X,X_j^-)}\), \(m(X)=\mathbb E[Y_j\mid X]\). All these random variables lie in \([a,b]\) where \(a=e^{-1/\tau}\) and \(b=e^{1/\tau}\). The normalized sum \(Q_M=M^{-1}\sum_jY_j+e^{s(X,Y)}/M\) is at least \(a\), as is \(m(X)\). The derivative of \(\log\) is bounded by \(1/a\) on this interval. Independence gives

\[\mathbb E\left|\frac1M\sum_jY_j-m(X)\right| \le\sqrt{\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}\!\left(M^{-1}\sum_jY_j\mid X\right)} \le\frac{b-a}{2\sqrt M}.\]

The variance bound uses that the squared deviation from the midpoint of \([a,b]\) is at most \((b-a)^2/4\), and variance minimizes mean squared deviation from a constant. Therefore

\[\left|\mathbb E\log Q_M-\mathbb E\log m(X)\right| \le a^{-1}\left(\frac{b-a}{2\sqrt M}+\frac bM\right).\]

The positive-pair term is independent of \(M\). This proves convergence and its rate. For unit embeddings \(s=1/\tau-\|f(x)-f(y)\|^2/(2\tau)\). The constants cancel, leaving

\[\frac{\mathbb E\|f(X)-f(Y)\|^2}{2\tau} +\mathbb E_X\log\mathbb E_Z e^{-\|f(X)-f(Z)\|^2/(2\tau)}.\]

The negative sample average converges for each anchor first. Jensen’s inequality gives \(\mathbb E_X\log m(X)\le\log\mathbb E_X m(X)\), with equality precisely when \(m(X)\) is almost surely constant. A pooled negative average inside a single logarithm therefore changes the objective in general.

Source of the challenge: Wang and Isola, Understanding Contrastive Representation Learning through Alignment and Uniformity on the Hypersphere, Theorem 1 and Appendix A.1. Original paper
