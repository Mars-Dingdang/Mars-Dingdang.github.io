ML 07: Support Vector Machines and Kernels

Derive hard- and soft-margin SVMs, use KKT conditions to recover the classifier, and prove positive semidefiniteness of the Gaussian kernel.

The closest points lie on the dashed support hyperplanes. The full margin is twice the distance from either hyperplane to the decision boundary.

From distance to a convex program

For \(f(x)=w^\top x+b\), the signed distance of a labeled point to the separating hyperplane is \(y_i f(x_i)/\|w\|\). Its formula follows by moving \(x_i\) along the unit normal until \(f\) is zero. Rescaling \((w,b)\) by a positive scalar leaves that geometric distance unchanged. On separated data, normalize \(\min_i y_if(x_i)=1\). Maximizing the minimum distance becomes

\[\min_{w,b}\tfrac12\|w\|^2 \quad\text{s.t. } y_i(w^\top x_i+b)\ge1\quad\forall i.\]

The supporting hyperplanes \(f=\pm1\) are \(2/\|w\|\) apart. For nonseparable data, introduce \(\xi_i\ge0\) and require \(y_if(x_i)\ge1-\xi_i\). Minimize \(\|w\|^2/2+C\sum_i\xi_i\). With \(w,b\) fixed, the best slack is \(\xi_i=(1-y_if(x_i))_+\), which yields the hinge-loss objective. Comparing with an average hinge loss plus \(\lambda\|w\|^2/2\) gives \(\lambda=1/(nC)\).

Deriving the dual

Consider first \(\min_{x\ge b} x^2\). For \(\alpha\ge0\), the Lagrangian \(x^2+\alpha(b-x)\) is at most \(x^2\) on the feasible set. Its infimum over all \(x\) is \(b\alpha-\alpha^2/4\), attained at \(x=\alpha/2\). Maximizing this concave function over \(\alpha\ge0\) gives \(\alpha^\star=\max(2b,0)\) and optimal value \(\max(b,0)^2\), matching the primal minimum at \(x^\star=\max(b,0)\). The KKT conditions are \(x\ge b\), \(\alpha\ge0\), \(2x=\alpha\), and \(\alpha(x-b)=0\). When \(b=0\), the constraint is active at zero even though its optimal multiplier vanishes.

With inequality multipliers \(\alpha_i,\beta_i\ge0\) for the margin and slack constraints, respectively, the Lagrangian is

\[\mathcal L=\tfrac12\|w\|^2+C\sum_i\xi_i+ \sum_i\alpha_i(1-\xi_i-y_i(w^\top x_i+b))-\sum_i\beta_i\xi_i.\]

For feasible primal variables, the added terms are nonpositive, so \(\inf_{w,b,\xi}\mathcal L\) is a lower bound on the primal optimum. Its infimum in \(b\) and \(\xi\) is finite only if their linear coefficients vanish. Minimizing in \(w\) completes the square. These calculations give

\[w=\sum_i\alpha_i y_i x_i,\qquad \sum_i\alpha_i y_i=0,\qquad C-\alpha_i-\beta_i=0.\]

Substitution gives

\[\max_{0\le\alpha_i\le C,\,\sum_i\alpha_i y_i=0} \left\{\sum_i\alpha_i-\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\alpha_j y_i y_j x_i^\top x_j\right\}.\]

Soft-margin strict feasibility follows from \(w=b=0,\xi_i=2\). Hard-margin strict feasibility follows by scaling any separator by a sufficiently large factor. Slater’s condition yields strong duality in either case. For the hard-margin problem there is no slack coefficient to impose an upper bound on \(\alpha_i\).

Complementary slackness gives \(\alpha_i(1-\xi_i-y_if(x_i))=0\) and \((C-\alpha_i)\xi_i=0\). Strictly outside the margin, \(\alpha_i=0\). An interior multiplier \(0<\alpha_i<C\) has zero slack and \(y_if(x_i)=1\), hence \(b=y_i-w^\top x_i\). At \(\alpha_i=C\), the point may be inside the margin or misclassified. If there are no interior multipliers, use the corresponding KKT inequalities to find the allowable interval for \(b\).

If strong duality holds and the optima are attained, \(d(\alpha^\star)\le\mathcal L(x^\star,\alpha^\star)\le f(x^\star)\) has equal endpoints. The first equality makes \(x^\star\) an unconstrained minimizer of the Lagrangian in the primal variables. The second makes each nonpositive product of a multiplier with its inequality vanish. This derives stationarity and complementary slackness without differentiating with respect to a multiplier at the boundary of its admissible range.

In the bias-free hard-margin case of slide 13, minimizing over \(w\) gives \(\sum_i\alpha_i-\|\sum_i\alpha_i y_i x_i\|^2/2\). The constraints are only \(\alpha_i\ge0\) because there is no free intercept. For a free intercept, let \(r_i=y_i-w^\top x_i\). If \(\alpha_i=0\), KKT requires \(b\ge r_i\) for \(y_i=1\) and \(b\le r_i\) for \(y_i=-1\). If \(\alpha_i=C\), these inequalities reverse because the point’s margin is at most one. Their intersection gives the allowable intercepts when there is no interior multiplier.

Kernels and finite Gram matrices

A feature map induces \(K(x,z)=\langle\phi(x),\phi(z)\rangle\). For any finite sample and real vector \(a\), the Gram matrix satisfies

\[a^\top K a=\left\|\sum_i a_i\phi(x_i)\right\|^2\ge0.\]

Conversely, a symmetric kernel whose every finite Gram matrix is positive semidefinite induces a feature space. Start with finitely supported formal sums of symbols \(\delta_x\), set \(\langle\delta_x,\delta_z\rangle=K(x,z)\), and extend bilinearly. Cauchy–Schwarz for this semidefinite form follows by requiring \(\|u+tv\|^2\ge0\) for all \(t\). Therefore zero-norm vectors are orthogonal to the whole space. Quotient them out, take the Hilbert completion, and map \(x\) to the equivalence class of \(\delta_x\). This construction suffices for the kernel trick. Mercer expansions additionally impose conditions on the integral operator and its domain.

The SVM dual replaces dot products by \(K\). The quadratic term has matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(y)K\operatorname{diag}(y)\), which remains positive semidefinite, so the maximization remains concave. The prediction is \(f(x)=\sum_i\alpha_i y_i K(x_i,x)+b\). The inhomogeneous quadratic feature map of slides 19–20 is

\[\phi(x)=\left(1,\,(\sqrt2 x_i)_i,\,(x_i^2)_i,\,(\sqrt2 x_ix_j)_{i<j}\right).\]

Its inner product expands to \(1+2\sum_i x_i z_i+\sum_i x_i^2z_i^2+2\sum_{i<j}x_ix_jz_iz_j\). The last two sums are \((x^\top z)^2\), so the whole expression equals \((1+x^\top z)^2\). Evaluating the latter takes \(O(d)\) operations despite the \(1+2d+d(d-1)/2\) explicit features. A dense Gram matrix evaluates at most \(n(n+1)/2\) distinct pairs by symmetry. Evaluation at a new point uses only kernels against support vectors.

For the Gaussian, write

\[K(x,z)=e^{-\|x\|^2/(2\sigma^2)}e^{-\|z\|^2/(2\sigma^2)} \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x^\top z)^m}{m!\sigma^{2m}}.\]

Each power is a kernel because \((x^\top z)^m=\langle x^{\otimes m},z^{\otimes m}\rangle\), including the constant kernel for \(m=0\). Partial sums have nonnegative Gram quadratic forms, and their finite-entry limits do also. Multiplying by the point factors applies the same diagonal scaling to both sides of the Gram matrix. This proves the Gaussian is a valid kernel.

Challenges

Problem 1

Strengthen the Gaussian proof: show its Gram matrix is positive definite for any finite set of pairwise distinct points and any finite \(\sigma>0\).

Show solution

If a quadratic form is zero, the preceding expansion expresses it as a sum of nonnegative squared norms. Writing \(b_i=a_i e^{-\|x_i\|^2/(2\sigma^2)}\), every tensor moment \(\sum_i b_i x_i^{\otimes m}\) must vanish. Pick a vector \(v\) such that the numbers \(s_i=v^\top x_i\) are all distinct. This is possible because the forbidden vectors lie in a finite union of proper hyperplanes. Contracting with \(v^{\otimes m}\) gives \(\sum_i b_is_i^m=0\) for \(m=0,\ldots,n-1\). The Vandermonde determinant is \(\prod_{i<j}(s_j-s_i)\ne0\), so all \(b_i\) and hence all \(a_i\) vanish.

Problem 2

Solve the one-dimensional soft-margin SVM for \((-1,-1)\) and \((1,1)\) at arbitrary \(C>0\). Which intercepts are optimal?

Show solution

The balance constraint gives \(\alpha_1=\alpha_2=\alpha\), and the dual objective is \(2\alpha-2\alpha^2\). Hence \(\alpha=\min(C,1/2)\) and \(w=2\alpha\). If \(C>1/2\), both multipliers are interior, so the margin equalities force \(b=0\). If \(C\le1/2\), the positive multipliers give \(\xi_1=1-w+b\) and \(\xi_2=1-w-b\). The feasible interval is \(|b|\le1-w\), reducing to zero at \(C=1/2\). All intercepts in the interval have the same total slack and satisfy KKT.

Paper challenge · Dual coordinates for squared hinge loss

Work in the bias-free linear formulation of Hsieh et al., “A Dual Coordinate Descent Method for Large-Scale Linear SVM,” §3. Starting from \(\min_w \|w\|_2^2/2+C\sum_i(1-y_iw^\top x_i)_+^2\), derive the dual diagonal correction, the exact projected coordinate update, and a certificate that every coordinate is optimal simultaneously. Explain how one sparse dot product and one sparse vector update replace a dense Gram row. Finally, prove geometric convergence in expectation when each coordinate is chosen uniformly at random.

Show paper solution

Introduce slack \(\xi_i\) with \(\xi_i\ge 1-y_iw^\top x_i\). Minimizing \(\xi_i^2\) over this half-line chooses \((1-y_iw^\top x_i)_+\), so a separate positivity constraint is unnecessary. With margin multipliers \(\alpha_i\ge0\), the unconstrained minimizers in the Lagrangian are \(w=\sum_i\alpha_i y_i x_i\) and \(\xi_i=\alpha_i/(2C)\). Thus the dual in minimization form is

\[F(\alpha)=\tfrac12\alpha^\top\left(Q+\tfrac1{2C}I\right)\alpha-\mathbf1^\top\alpha, \qquad Q_{ij}=y_i y_j x_i^\top x_j,\quad \alpha_i\ge0.\]

Strong duality follows from strict primal feasibility. Set \(D=Q+I/(2C)\). Along coordinate \(i\), the objective variation for change \(d\) is \(G_i d+D_{ii}d^2/2\), with \(G_i=y_iw^\top x_i+\alpha_i/(2C)-1\) and \(d\ge-\alpha_i\). Completing the square gives \(\alpha_i^+=\max(0,\alpha_i-G_i/D_{ii})\) with \(D_{ii}=\|x_i\|_2^2+1/(2C)>0\). Apply the update \(w^+=w+(\alpha_i^+-\alpha_i)y_i x_i\). Each operation visits only the nonzero entries of \(x_i\).

For all coordinates define the projected derivative as \(G_i\) if \(\alpha_i>0\) and \(\min(G_i,0)\) at zero. Its simultaneous vanishing is necessary by feasible one-dimensional derivatives. It is sufficient because then \(\nabla F(\alpha)^\top(\beta-\alpha)\ge0\) for every \(\beta\ge0\), and convexity gives \(F(\beta)\ge F(\alpha)\). The diagonal correction also makes \(F\) strongly convex and its dual optimizer unique. For ordinary hinge loss, the slack term is linear, yielding \(0\le\alpha_i\le C\), zero diagonal correction, and clipping at both endpoints. A free intercept imposes \(\sum_i y_i\alpha_i=0\) and generally requires coordinated changes to two multipliers.

For the convergence proof, let \(\mu=1/(2C)>0\), \(L_{\max}=\max_i D_{ii}\), and \(s=\alpha^\star-\alpha\). For \(0\le t\le1\), \(\alpha+t s_i e_i\) is a feasible coordinate step, so the exact coordinate update at \(i\) achieves at least as much decrease. Conditional on the current iterate and averaging the quadratic expansions over uniform \(i\) gives

\[\mathbb E_i[F(\alpha^+)-F(\alpha)] \le\frac tn\nabla F(\alpha)^\top s+\frac{t^2L_{\max}}{2n}\|s\|^2.\]

Strong convexity implies \(\nabla F(\alpha)^\top s\le F(\alpha^\star)-F(\alpha)-\mu\|s\|^2/2\). Choose \(t=\mu/L_{\max}\le1\) so the squared terms cancel. The gap contracts in conditional expectation by \(1-\mu/(nL_{\max})\) per step, and repeated expectations give geometric convergence. This proof uses the positive diagonal correction in the squared-hinge dual.
